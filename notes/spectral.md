Compiled 2026-07-23.

## 1. The Hilbert–Pólya conjecture (origins)

**The Odlyzko–Pólya correspondence** — A. M. Odlyzko & G. Pólya, letters of 8 Dec 1981, 3 Jan 1982, 18 Jan 1982, 26 Apr 1982.
Link: https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/polya/index.html (scans of all letters). Not downloaded (image scans; page recorded).
This is the *only documented source* for the "Hilbert–Pólya conjecture." Pólya, replying to Odlyzko, recalls that in Göttingen around 1914 Landau asked him whether there was a physical reason RH should be true; Pólya answered that RH would follow "if the nontrivial zeros of the Xi-function were so connected with a physical problem that the Riemann hypothesis would be equivalent to the fact that all the eigenvalues of the physical problem are real." He adds: "I never published this remark, but somehow it became known." Odlyzko separately wrote to Olga Taussky-Todd (19 Jan 1982) hunting for Hilbert's half of the attribution; she could supply nothing, so Hilbert's association with the conjecture is purely anecdotal. The modern statement: the imaginary parts t_n of the nontrivial zeros 1/2 + i t_n are the spectrum of some self-adjoint (or otherwise reality-forcing) operator.
**Status**: not a theorem or even a precise conjecture — a program. Everything below is an attempt to give it content.
**Assessment**: As a strategy it is coherent (self-adjointness ⇒ real spectrum ⇒ RH), and it has one genuine precedent (Weil/Deligne in function fields, where zeros *are* Frobenius eigenvalues). The obstruction is that nobody has produced an operator whose spectrum is *provably* the zeros without assuming what is to be proved.

## 2. Montgomery's pair correlation and the Dyson connection

**The pair correlation of zeros of the zeta function** — H. L. Montgomery, 1973, in *Analytic Number Theory*, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. XXIV (AMS), pp. 181–193.
Link: https://public.websites.umich.edu/~hlm/paircor1.pdf (author's scan). Local: `Montgomery-1973-pair-correlation.pdf`.
Assuming RH, Montgomery studied the form factor F(α, T) of the zeros and proved its behavior rigorously for |α| < 1, conjecturing F(α) = 1 for |α| ≥ 1; equivalently, the pair correlation of normalized zero spacings should be 1 − (sin πu / πu)². As a by-product he proved (on RH) that at least 2/3 of the zeros are simple. The famous teatime conversation at the IAS (spring 1972): Freeman Dyson instantly recognized 1 − (sin πu/πu)² as the pair-correlation function of eigenvalues of large random GUE matrices — the birth of the zeta–random-matrix connection and the strongest circumstantial evidence that the zeros are eigenvalues of *something* in the GUE (broken time-reversal) universality class.
**Status**: the |α| < 1 range is proved (on RH); the full conjecture is open — extending F(α) past |α| = 1 encodes deep prime-pair (Hardy–Littlewood) information. Rudnick–Sarnak (Duke Math. J. 81, 1996, 269–322 — paywalled, citation only) extended restricted-range universality to n-level correlations and to general automorphic L-functions.
**Assessment**: This is evidence, not mechanism. Pair correlation cannot by itself prove RH (it is a statement *about* the zeros' statistics, computed assuming RH), but it constrains any candidate operator: it must be in the GUE class, chaotic, without time-reversal.

## 3. Odlyzko's numerics

**On the distribution of spacings between zeros of the zeta function** — A. M. Odlyzko, 1987, Math. Comp. 48, 273–308.
Link: https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/arch/zeta.zero.spacing.pdf. Local: `Odlyzko-1987-zero-spacings.pdf`.

**The 10^20-th zero of the Riemann zeta function and 175 million of its neighbors** — A. M. Odlyzko, 1992, unpublished manuscript (163 pp.).
Link: https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/unpublished/zeta.10to20.1992.pdf. Local: `Odlyzko-1992-10to20-zero.pdf`.
Odlyzko computed millions of zeros at enormous heights (near zero #10^12 in 1987, #10^20 in 1992, later #10^22) using the Odlyzko–Schönhage algorithm, and compared nearest-neighbor spacings, pair correlations, and number variance against GUE predictions. Agreement is spectacular and improves with height; moreover the *deviations* from GUE at long range are exactly those predicted by Berry's semiclassical theory, with the primes playing the role of periodic orbits (number variance saturates where the "orbit" of period log 2 cuts off the universality regime). The 1992 manuscript is the canonical reference for the "Montgomery–Odlyzko law."
**Status**: numerics only, but the tightest empirical support the Hilbert–Pólya picture has; no counter-signal has ever appeared.
**Assessment**: Numerics prove nothing about RH, but the prime-by-prime match of GUE *deviations* is the strongest hint that a genuine semiclassical dynamical system underlies zeta — it tells us the hypothetical dynamics has periodic orbits of period log p^k with instability exponents matching p^{k/2}.

## 4. Berry (1986) and Berry–Keating: the H = xp program

**Riemann's zeta function: a model for quantum chaos?** — M. V. Berry, 1986, in *Quantum Chaos and Statistical Nuclear Physics* (Springer Lecture Notes in Physics 263).
Link: https://michaelberryphysics.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/berry154.pdf. Local: `Berry-1986-zeta-model-quantum-chaos.pdf`.
Berry proposed reading the Riemann–von Mangoldt explicit formula as a Gutzwiller trace formula: primes p ↔ primitive periodic orbits with periods log p, repetitions p^k, amplitudes p^{−k/2} ↔ exponential orbit instability. The hypothetical "Riemann dynamics" must be chaotic, homogeneously unstable, and *lack time-reversal symmetry* (to get GUE, not GOE); the absence of a factor-2 degeneracy and the sign structure are diagnostics. This paper set the entire quantum-chaos agenda for zeta.

**H = xp and the Riemann zeros** — M. V. Berry & J. P. Keating, 1999, in *Supersymmetry and Trace Formulae: Chaos and Disorder* (NATO ASI, Kluwer), pp. 355–367.
Link: https://michaelberryphysics.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/berry306.pdf; https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4615-4875-1_19. Local: `Berry-1999-H-xp-riemann-zeros.pdf`.

**The Riemann zeros and eigenvalue asymptotics** — M. V. Berry & J. P. Keating, 1999, SIAM Review 41(2), 236–266.
Link: https://michaelberryphysics.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/berry307.pdf. Local: `Berry-1999-eigenvalue-asymptotics-SIAM.pdf`.
The observation: for the classical Hamiltonian H = xp, the phase-space area under the hyperbola xp = E with Planck-cell cutoffs x > l_x, p > l_p (l_x l_p = 2πħ) gives N(E) ≈ (E/2π)(log(E/2π) − 1) + 7/8 — precisely the smooth Riemann zero-counting function, including the constant 7/8. The dilation generator (xp + px)/2 is essentially self-adjoint on the half-line but has purely *continuous* spectrum ℝ (it is −i d/dτ in Mellin/log variables), so the zeros must arise from an unknown quantization/boundary condition that discretizes it; the truncation that produces the counting function is not a self-adjoint boundary condition. The SIAM Review paper also develops the striking analogy between the Riemann–Siegel formula and semiclassical spectral determinants (resurgence), arguing zeta behaves like the spectral determinant of a chaotic system exactly at the border of universality.
**Status**: the mean spectral density matches exactly; the fluctuating part (the zeros themselves) was never obtained; H = xp is a caricature — its classical dynamics is too simple (one hyperbolic orbit family, unbounded, no chaos, no primes).
**Assessment**: The most influential wrong-but-fruitful idea in the field. The key obstructions: (i) regularizing xp to get a discrete spectrum destroys the connection to primes; (ii) nothing arithmetic ever enters. A real proof would need the arithmetic (Euler product / explicit formula) built into the operator, not just the mean density.

**A compact hamiltonian with the same asymptotic mean spectral density as the Riemann zeros** — M. V. Berry & J. P. Keating, 2011, J. Phys. A 44, 285203.
Link: https://michaelberryphysics.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/berry4401.pdf; https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1751-8113/44/28/285203. Local: `Berry-2011-compact-hamiltonian.pdf`.
Response to Sierra–Rodríguez-Laguna (below): the classical Hamiltonian (x + 1/x)(p + 1/p) has all orbits bounded; after symmetrization the quantum problem becomes a compact integral operator on x > 0 with a one-parameter family of self-adjoint extensions (angle α), a discrete real spectrum, and the first two terms of the eigenvalue density agree with the Riemann counting function. The spectrum winds as a single helix on the (E, α) cylinder.
**Status**: a genuine self-adjoint operator with the right *mean* density — but its fluctuation statistics are not the zeta zeros', and again no primes appear.
**Assessment**: Demonstrates how cheap the mean density is (many operators achieve it) and therefore how far mean-density matching is from Hilbert–Pólya. The gap between "right smooth counting" and "right zeros" is exactly where all xp-type models die.

## 5. Connes: adele class space, trace formula, and the prolate operator

**Trace formula in noncommutative geometry and the zeros of the Riemann zeta function** — A. Connes, 1998/1999, Selecta Math. (N.S.) 5, 29–106; arXiv:math/9811068.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811068. Local: `Connes-1998-trace-formula-ncg.pdf`.
Connes constructs the adele class space X = A_Q/Q^× with the scaling action of the idele class group C_Q and shows the zeta zeros appear as an *absorption* spectrum: in a suitable Sobolev-type function space on X, the zeros on the critical line label the missing lines (a cokernel), rather than an emission spectrum. He proves a semi-local trace formula and shows that the validity of a global trace formula of the same shape is *equivalent* to RH — the trace formula's spectral side reproduces the Weil explicit formula, and RH becomes Weil's positivity criterion for the explicit-formula distribution. This is Hilbert–Pólya turned inside out: the space is natural and arithmetic (adelic), the operator is the scaling flow, and the primes enter automatically via the orbits of the flow (periods log p — exactly Berry's wish list). H = xp is visibly the archimedean germ of this picture.
**Status**: spectral *interpretation* achieved unconditionally (also in later Connes–Consani reformulations); the *proof* stalls exactly at establishing global positivity — the problem is displaced, not solved. Connes himself has said the difficulty becomes proving an analogue of the Riemann–Roch/positivity step that Weil used in function fields.
**Assessment**: The most serious, structurally faithful realization of Hilbert–Pólya: it explains *why* primes ↔ periodic orbits and why the spectrum is absorption. But equivalence-to-RH is a double-edged sword: nothing has been gained until positivity is proven, and 25+ years of effort (see below) have produced only the archimedean/semi-local case.

**An essay on the Riemann Hypothesis** — A. Connes, 2015, in *Open Problems in Mathematics* (Springer, 2016); arXiv:1509.05576.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05576. Local: `Connes-2015-essay-on-RH.pdf`.
Candid survey of the three strategies (RH as spectral, RH as positivity, RH via tropical/characteristic-one geometry) and an honest post-mortem of why the naive analogies fail; the best single "state of the program" document, including the scaling site and the search for a Riemann–Roch strategy imitating Weil's function-field proof.

**Weil positivity and trace formula: the archimedean place** — A. Connes & C. Consani, 2020/2021, Selecta Math. 27, 77; arXiv:2006.13771.
Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13771. Local: `ConnesConsani-2020-weil-positivity.pdf`.
Proves Weil positivity for test functions supported near the archimedean place (support in (2^{-1/2}, 2^{1/2})), using the trace formula, quantized calculus, and prolate functions — i.e., RH "holds locally at ∞." First genuinely positive result in the positivity program.

**Prolate spheroidal operator and zeta** — A. Connes & H. Moscovici, 2021/2022, PNAS 119, e2123174119; arXiv:2112.05500. Local: `Connes-2021-prolate-spheroidal-zeta.pdf`.
**Zeta zeros and prolate wave operators** — A. Connes, C. Consani & H. Moscovici, 2023/2024, arXiv:2310.18423 (J. Noncommut. Geom., to appear). Local: `Connes-2023-zeta-prolate-wave.pdf`.
The self-adjoint extension of the classical prolate spheroidal (time-band-limiting) operator, restricted to the complement of an interval, has negative eigenvalues whose ultraviolet asymptotics match the *squares* of the zeta zeros; the 2023 paper builds a semi-local prolate wave operator that also captures the low-lying zeros, tying the prolate operator into the semi-local trace formula and Sonin spaces. This is the closest anyone has come to exhibiting a concrete, classical, self-adjoint differential operator whose spectrum provably tracks the zeros asymptotically.
**Status**: active frontier (Connes' current main line, with a stated "expectation of a spectral realization" merging UV and IR); the match is asymptotic, and turning it into an exact spectral realization plus positivity is open.
**Assessment**: If any spectral route yields a proof this decade, it is this one — it is arithmetic, self-adjoint, and unconditional as far as it goes. The obstruction remains converting asymptotic spectral agreement into the exact global positivity statement equivalent to RH.

## 6. Bender–Brody–Müller and its critiques

**Hamiltonian for the zeros of the Riemann zeta function** — C. M. Bender, D. C. Brody & M. P. Müller, 2017, Phys. Rev. Lett. 118, 130201; arXiv:1608.03679. Local: `Bender-2017-hamiltonian-zeta-zeros.pdf`.
BBM define Ĥ = Δ^{-1}(x̂p̂ + p̂x̂)Δ with Δ = 1 − e^{−ip̂} (a non-unitary similarity transform of the Berry–Keating dilation operator). Formal eigenfunctions are Hurwitz zeta functions ψ_z(x) = −ζ(z, x+1) with eigenvalue i(2z − 1); imposing the boundary condition ψ_z(0) = 0 forces ζ(z) = 0, so eigenvalues ↔ nontrivial zeros. iĤ is PT-symmetric; the authors argue that if PT symmetry is unbroken (equivalently, a formal pseudo-Hermiticity/biorthogonality argument goes through) the eigenvalues are real, "suggesting" RH; they also propose a rigorous reduced statement ("local RH"-style) on a half-line.
**Critiques**:
- **Comment on "Hamiltonian for the zeros..."** — J. V. Bellissard, 2017, arXiv:1704.02644. Local: `Bellissard-2017-comment-BBM.pdf`. Points out (i) p̂ on the half-line admits no self-adjoint extension, so the ingredients are ill-defined; (ii) the eigenfunctions ζ(z, x+1) on Re z = 1/2 are not square-integrable, so "eigenvalue" has no Hilbert-space meaning; concludes the reality argument does not work as a strategy for RH.
- **Reply** — Bender, Brody & Müller, 2017, arXiv:1705.06767. Local: `Bender-2017-reply-to-bellissard.pdf`. Concede the operator is not Hermitian on a standard L² space and the eigenfunctions are non-normalizable, but argue these points were acknowledged in the Letter and the heuristic (weighted inner products, analogy with PT-symmetric quantum mechanics) stands as a program.
- **A note on "Hamiltonian for the zeros..."** — M. P. Müller, 2017, arXiv:1704.04705. Local: `Muller-2017-note-on-BBM.pdf`. Müller's own careful follow-up: constructs a rigorous version of the operator on a space of "asymptotically flat" functions, clarifying exactly which parts of the PRL are theorems and which are formal.
**Status**: widely publicized, not accepted as progress; the reality of the spectrum is exactly as open as RH. No arithmetic input beyond the boundary condition "ζ(z) = 0", which is where all the content hides.
**Assessment**: Instructive failure mode: a similarity transform of xp can encode the zeros in a boundary condition, but the operator is non-self-adjoint and the similarity is unbounded, so no reality theorem follows. Any descendant of this idea must first solve the functional-analytic problem (which inner product makes Ĥ self-adjoint?) — and that question is plausibly RH-hard itself.

## 7. Germán Sierra's body of work

**Landau levels and Riemann zeros** — G. Sierra & P. K. Townsend, 2008, Phys. Rev. Lett. 101, 110201; arXiv:0805.4079. Local: `Sierra-2008-landau-levels.pdf`.
A charged particle in the xy-plane in a uniform magnetic field plus saddle electrostatic potential xy: in the lowest Landau level the effective Hamiltonian is exactly xp, and the smooth zero-counting function (including 7/8) emerges from the Landau-level problem. First concrete physical arena for Berry–Keating, and origin of the "zeros as quantum Hall spectra" idea.

**H = xp model revisited and the Riemann zeros** — G. Sierra & J. Rodríguez-Laguna, 2011, Phys. Rev. Lett. 106, 200201; arXiv:1102.5356. Local: `Sierra-2011-xp-revisited.pdf`.
Modifies to H = x(p + l_p²/p): classical orbits become closed, the quantum spectrum is discrete, and semiclassically N(E) reproduces the Riemann counting including the 7/8 Maslov-type constant. Triggered Berry–Keating 2011 (above).

**General covariant xp models and the Riemann zeros** — G. Sierra, 2012, J. Phys. A 45, 055209; arXiv:1110.3203. Local: `Sierra-2011-covariant-xp-models.pdf`.
Shows the xp-type models are general-covariant 1D systems equivalent to a massive Dirac fermion in Rindler spacetime — the relativistic reformulation that underlies his later constructions (xp ↔ Rindler Hamiltonian/boost generator, another echo of Connes' scaling flow and of the Bisognano–Wichmann modular picture).

**The Riemann zeros as spectrum and the Riemann hypothesis: a review** — G. Sierra, 2016 (v4 2019), Symmetry 11, 494; arXiv:1601.01797. Local: `Sierra-2016-riemann-zeros-spectrum-review.pdf`.
The definitive review of the whole Hilbert–Pólya/physics program (Pólya's and Selberg's precedents, xp and all its regularizations, Landau models, Dirac-in-Rindler models, Connes, BBM, and his own culminating model): a massless Dirac fermion in Rindler spacetime with delta-function potentials ("moving mirrors") localized at log of the square-free integers, with amplitudes given by the Möbius function; tuning a self-adjoint-extension phase to the phase of zeta on the critical line makes zeros on the line into bound states in the transparent-mirror limit — a "physicist's proof" sketch, plus an interferometer proposal for observing zeros. Also covers his earlier cyclic-renormalization-group connection (Russian-doll BCS; arXiv:math/0510572).
**Status**: the array-of-mirrors model builds the primes in (via Möbius amplitudes) — the only xp-descendant that does — but the crucial step (that the tuned self-adjoint extension exists *without assuming* where the zeros are, and that off-line zeros are excluded) is heuristic; nothing is proven at mathematical standard.
**Assessment**: Sierra has pushed the physics program as far as it goes short of theorems. The persistent obstruction: models either have the right mean density with no primes, or have primes inserted by hand with self-adjointness contingent on the phase of zeta itself — circularity lurks exactly at the RH-hard step.

## 8. Srednicki and other Hamiltonian models

**The Berry–Keating Hamiltonian and the local Riemann hypothesis** — M. Srednicki, 2011, J. Phys. A 44, 305202; arXiv:1104.1850. Local: `Srednicki-2011-berry-keating-local-RH.pdf`.
The "local RH" (Bump–Ng–Choi): zeros of Mellin transforms of harmonic-oscillator eigenfunctions lie on Re s = 1/2. Srednicki shows those zeros are eigenvalues of the Berry–Keating operator (xp + px)/2 projected onto the subspace spanned by lower oscillator eigenfunctions — an honest, complete *spectral proof of a local RH*, in exactly the Hilbert–Pólya spirit, including a p-adic version. Shows the mechanism can work when the function is simple enough.

**Nonclassical degrees of freedom in the Riemann Hamiltonian** — M. Srednicki, 2011, Phys. Rev. Lett. 107, 100201; arXiv:1105.2342. Local: `Srednicki-2011-nonclassical-dof.pdf`.
If the Katz–Sarnak "symplectic" symmetry assignment for zeta (from its family/low-lying-zero behavior) is taken seriously, the Riemann Hamiltonian sits in Altland–Zirnbauer class C, which requires a nonclassical two-valued (spin-like) degree of freedom; periodic orbits then contribute with an extra (−1), resolving the long-standing wrong sign of the oscillatory terms in the zero density vs. Gutzwiller (Berry's "sign problem" — primes contribute with the opposite sign to a naive chaotic system). A sharp structural constraint on any candidate dynamics (see also his 2021 SCGP talk "Katz–Sarnak families and the Riemann Hamiltonian").

**The Hamiltonian H = xp and classification of osp(1|2) representations** — G. Regniers & J. Van der Jeugt, 2010, AIP Conf. Proc. 1243, 138; arXiv:1001.1285. Local: `Regniers-2010-xp-osp12.pdf`.
Wigner quantization of H = xp: the problem maps to unitary irreps of the Lie superalgebra osp(1|2), whose classification yields spectra that can be all of R, a half-line, or *equidistant discrete* — demonstrating that "the spectrum of xp" is radically quantization-scheme-dependent. No arithmetic content; its value is the no-go moral: without extra structure, xp does not know about zeta.
**Related, recorded but not downloaded**: Wu–Sprung potential (H. Wu & D. W. L. Sprung, Phys. Rev. E 48 (1993) 2595 — inverse-scattering 1D potential with fractal dimension ~1.5 whose smooth spectrum matches the first zeros; paywalled); Berry–Keating-operator-on-a-lattice, arXiv:1610.06472.

## 9. Random-matrix moments: Keating–Snaith and CFKRS

**Random matrix theory and ζ(1/2 + it)** — J. P. Keating & N. C. Snaith, 2000, Comm. Math. Phys. 214, 57–89.
Link: https://people.maths.bris.ac.uk/~mancs/papers/RMTzeta.pdf. Local: `KeatingSnaith-2000-RMT-zeta.pdf`.
Models ζ on the critical line by the characteristic polynomial of a random CUE/GUE matrix: exact moments of |Λ| via Selberg's integral give the conjecture that the 2k-th moment of |ζ(1/2+it)| grows like a(k)·g(k)·(log T)^{k²} with g(k) = G²(k+1)/G(2k+1) (Barnes G), the arithmetic factor a(k) glued in by hand. Reproduces Hardy–Littlewood (k=1) and Ingham (k=2), and predicts Conrey–Ghosh's 42 (k=3) and Conrey–Gonek's 24024 (k=4). Companion paper: "Random matrix theory and L-functions at s = 1/2" (same volume). The moments conjecture is the quantitative core of the "zeta = characteristic polynomial of a random/chaotic operator" picture.

**Integral moments of L-functions** — J. B. Conrey, D. W. Farmer, J. P. Keating, M. O. Rubinstein & N. C. Snaith (CFKRS), 2005, Proc. London Math. Soc. 91, 33–104; arXiv:math/0206018. Local: `CFKRS-2002-integral-moments.pdf`.
The "CFKRS recipe": full conjectural asymptotic expansions (polynomials of degree k², all lower-order terms) for shifted moments of zeta and of families of L-functions of each symmetry type, derived from a heuristic approximate-functional-equation calculus; spectacularly confirmed numerically and, in low moments and function fields, rigorously.
**Status**: conjectures; rigorous only for k = 1, 2 (zeta), with breakthroughs on upper/lower bounds of the right order (Soundararajan, Harper) assuming RH. Function-field analogues proven in many cases.
**Assessment**: This line is about the *consequences* and calibration of the RMT dictionary, not a route to RH. Its role in a future proof: any spectral model must reproduce the arithmetic factor a(k), which is precisely what pure RMT lacks — the recurring "primes don't appear" obstruction in its sharpest quantitative form.

## 10. Fyodorov–Hiary–Keating: extreme values

**Freezing transition, characteristic polynomials of random matrices, and the Riemann zeta function** — Y. V. Fyodorov, G. A. Hiary & J. P. Keating, 2012, Phys. Rev. Lett. 108, 170601; arXiv:1202.4713. Local: `Fyodorov-2012-freezing-extreme-values.pdf`.
**Freezing transitions and extreme values: random matrix theory, ζ(1/2 + it), and disordered landscapes** — Y. V. Fyodorov & J. P. Keating, 2014, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 372, 20120503; arXiv:1211.6063. Local: `Fyodorov-2013-freezing-review.pdf`.
Treats log|ζ| on a short interval of the critical line as a log-correlated Gaussian field (like branching random walk / 2D GFF / GMC) and imports the statistical-mechanics "freezing" phenomenon to conjecture: max over [T, T+2π] of log|ζ(1/2+it)| = log log T − (3/4) log log log T + O(1), with a specific fluctuation distribution.
**Status**: essentially *proven*: leading order by Najnudel (2018) and by Arguin–Belius–Bourgade–Radziwiłł–Soundararajan (CPAM 72, 2019; arXiv:1612.08575, local: `Arguin-2016-maximum-zeta-short-interval.pdf`), and the −3/4 log log log T term by Arguin–Bourgade–Radziwiłł (2020–2023). One of the very few RMT-inspired conjectures about zeta with a full rigorous confirmation.
**Assessment**: A genuine, completed success of the physics dictionary — but a local/statistical statement, agnostic about RH. Its meta-lesson for the program: physics heuristics about zeta *can* be turned into theorems when the statement lives in the "universal" (non-arithmetic) regime; RH itself does not.

## 11. Katz–Sarnak symmetry

**Zeroes of zeta functions and symmetry** — N. M. Katz & P. Sarnak, 1999, Bull. AMS 36, 1–26.
Link: https://www.ams.org/journals/bull/1999-36-01/S0273-0979-99-00766-1/S0273-0979-99-00766-1.pdf. Local: `KatzSarnak-1999-zeroes-symmetry.pdf`. (Companion monograph: *Random Matrices, Frobenius Eigenvalues, and Monodromy*, AMS 1999.)
In the function-field world, RH is a theorem (Weil, Deligne) precisely because zeros *are* eigenvalues — of Frobenius acting on cohomology — and Katz–Sarnak proved that as one averages over families of curves/varieties over finite fields, the zeros' statistics converge to the eigenvalue statistics of the classical compact groups determined by the family's monodromy group (U, O, USp — the "symmetry types"). They conjectured the same trichotomy governs low-lying zeros of families of number-field L-functions (verified in many cases, unconditionally in restricted ranges: Iwaniec–Luo–Sarnak etc.). This is the only setting where the full Hilbert–Pólya logic — operator, self-adjointness/unitarity, positivity, proof of RH — has actually been carried to completion.
**Status**: theorems in function fields; conjectures (with strong partial results) for number fields. The "missing Frobenius"/missing cohomology for Spec Z is the acknowledged gap — the motivation for Connes' site, Deninger's foliated dynamical systems, and F_1 geometry.
**Assessment**: The deepest reason to believe the spectral philosophy. The obstruction is not analytic but geometric: constructing the cohomology theory (a "Weil cohomology for Spec Z") in which zeta's zeros are eigenvalues, with a positivity (Castelnuovo/Hodge-index-type) inequality. That is likely equivalent in difficulty to RH itself, but at least it is a *structured* difficulty.

## 12. Arithmetic quantum chaos and the Selberg analogy

**Arithmetic quantum chaos (Schur Lectures)** — P. Sarnak, 1995, Israel Math. Conf. Proc. 8, 183–236 (Schur/Blyth Lectures, Tel Aviv 1992).
Link: https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/Arithmetic%20Quantum%20Chaos.pdf. Local: `Sarnak-1995-arithmetic-quantum-chaos.pdf`.
Quantum chaos on arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces: Maass forms as chaotic eigenstates, QUE, and the surprise that arithmetic surfaces' spectra show *Poissonian* (not GOE) spacings because of exponentially large Hecke degeneracies of geodesic lengths — a warning that arithmetic systems violate naive universality. Directly relevant to RH because these Laplacians are the working prototype of a Hilbert–Pólya operator (for Selberg zeta functions), and because the Hecke-operator structure is what any "Riemann operator" would need an analogue of.

**Selberg's trace formula: an introduction** — J. Marklof, 2004/2011, in *Hyperbolic Geometry and Applications in Quantum Chaos and Cosmology* (CUP); arXiv:math/0407288. Local: `Marklof-2004-selberg-trace-formula-intro.pdf`. (Primary historical reference, paywalled, citation only: D. A. Hejhal, "The Selberg trace formula and the Riemann zeta function," Duke Math. J. 43 (1976) 441–482; also Selberg 1956.)
The Selberg zeta function Z(s) of a compact hyperbolic surface satisfies its own "Riemann hypothesis" (nontrivial zeros at s = 1/2 + i r_n, up to finitely many exceptional ones): *proven*, because λ_n = 1/4 + r_n² are eigenvalues of the self-adjoint Laplace–Beltrami operator, and the Selberg trace formula relates them to the length spectrum (primitive closed geodesics ↔ "primes," lengths ↔ log p). The Riemann–Weil explicit formula is formally *identical* to a trace formula for an unknown system with orbits of length log p — Hejhal's paper is the classic dissection of how close and how far the analogy is (e.g., the multiplicities and the sign work out "as if" the system existed, but the hyperbolic surface that would have geodesic lengths exactly {k log p} does not exist; the analogue would need "half a Laplacian" / a system at ħ-independent border of universality).
**Status**: the analogy is precise and is the template for both Berry–Keating (semiclassics) and Connes (trace formula); transplanting it requires the missing geometric object — same gap as Katz–Sarnak.
**Assessment**: The Selberg case proves the Hilbert–Pólya mechanism is not a fantasy: it fully works for a cousin zeta. Everything missing for Riemann zeta is concentrated in one question: *what dynamical/geometric object has the primes as its length spectrum?* (Connes' adele class space is the leading candidate answer; Deninger's program is the other.)

## 13. Experimental realizations of Riemann zeros as spectra

**Finding zeros of the Riemann zeta function by periodic driving of cold atoms** — C. E. Creffield & G. Sierra, 2015, Phys. Rev. A 91, 063608; arXiv:1411.0459. Local: `Creffield-2014-cold-atoms-zeros.pdf`.
Proposal: coherent destruction of tunneling for a cold atom in a driven optical lattice, with a driving waveform engineered so tunneling freezes exactly when a driving parameter hits a zeta zero — zeros become experimentally detectable dynamical features. Follow-up single-qubit version: Sierra et al., arXiv:1903.07819.

**Riemann zeros from Floquet engineering a trapped-ion qubit** — R. He, M.-Z. Ai, J.-M. Cui, Y.-F. Huang, Y.-J. Han, C.-F. Li, G.-C. Guo, G. Sierra, C. E. Creffield, 2021, npj Quantum Information 7, 109; arXiv:2102.06936. Local: `He-2021-trapped-ion-riemann-zeros.pdf`.
Realization of the above: a ¹⁷¹Yb⁺ qubit in a Paul trap, microwave-driven; the first 80 nontrivial zeros measured as freezing points of the quasienergy spectrum, in excellent agreement with theory. Other physical embodiments: Landau-level/quantum-Hall proposals (Sierra–Townsend), Sierra's interferometer (in the 2019 review), light-scattering computation of zeros (PRB 110, L220201 (2024)).
**Status**: real experiments — but they *encode* known zeros into engineered drives; the zeta function is input, not output. No experiment probes an arithmetic Hamiltonian.
**Assessment**: Zero direct value for proving RH; real value as a demonstration that zeros are physically realizable spectra and as technology for analog computation. A proof-relevant experiment would require a system whose Hamiltonian is *forced by nature* to know the primes, which none of these are.

## 14. Surveys

**Colloquium: Physics of the Riemann hypothesis** — D. Schumayer & D. A. W. Hutchinson, 2011, Rev. Mod. Phys. 83, 307; arXiv:1101.3116. Local: `Schumayer-2011-physics-of-RH.pdf`.
Panoramic survey of every physics–zeta contact point: Hilbert–Pólya, GUE statistics, xp, quantum chaos, billiards, statistical mechanics (Lee–Yang, primon gas/Julia), inverse-scattering Wu–Sprung potentials, cellular constructions. Good map of the territory and of dead branches (e.g., Lee–Yang-style approaches never localized zeta zeros).
Also relevant: Scholarpedia "Riemann zeros and quantum chaos" (Keating); Bourgade–Keating Poincaré-seminar survey.

## Overall assessment of the spectral program

The spectral program is the only approach to RH with a completed proof-of-concept in a neighboring world: for function fields (Weil, Deligne) and for Selberg zeta functions, zeros really are eigenvalues of a natural operator (Frobenius on cohomology; the Laplacian on a hyperbolic surface), and RH-type statements follow from self-adjointness/unitarity plus a positivity inequality. For Riemann's zeta itself, fifty years of work have produced overwhelming *evidence* for the picture — Montgomery's pair correlation, Odlyzko's numerics matching GUE down to the prime-driven correction terms, the Keating–Snaith/CFKRS moment dictionary, and the fully proven Fyodorov–Hiary–Keating extreme-value conjecture — together with increasingly refined *constraints* on the hypothetical "Riemann operator": it must be quantum-chaotic without time-reversal symmetry (GUE), possess periodic orbits of periods log p^k with instabilities p^{k/2} entering with an anomalous minus sign (Berry, Srednicki's class-C argument), have mean spectral density (E/2π)(log(E/2π) − 1) + 7/8 (Berry–Keating's xp), and sit exactly at the border of semiclassical universality (Riemann–Siegel resurgence). What it has *not* produced is the operator. Every explicit Hamiltonian either gets the smooth counting function with no arithmetic (xp and all its regularizations — Sierra, Berry–Keating 2011, Landau models), or inserts the arithmetic by hand at the RH-hard step (Sierra's Möbius mirrors, BBM's boundary condition ζ(z) = 0 on a non-self-adjoint operator), or is rigorous but local/toy (Srednicki's local RH). For Hilbert–Pólya to become a proof, three things must happen at once: (1) a natural space with arithmetic content on which a flow/operator acts — Connes' adele class space is the only known candidate where primes-as-periodic-orbits comes out rather than being put in; (2) an exact (not asymptotic, not mean-density) identification of the spectrum with the zeros — achieved by Connes only as an absorption spectrum, and by Connes–Moscovici's prolate operator only in UV asymptotics; and (3) a positivity/self-adjointness theorem that does not presuppose knowledge of where the zeros are.

The standard criticisms therefore remain in force. First, "no natural operator": self-adjointness is cheap (any real sequence is the spectrum of some self-adjoint operator), so the content of Hilbert–Pólya is entirely in the word *natural* — the operator must be defined independently of zeta and provably tied to it, and no xp-descendant, PT-symmetric construction, or engineered experimental spectrum has crossed that bar; the quantization ambiguity exposed by Regniers–Van der Jeugt shows "quantize xp" is not even a well-posed instruction. Second, "equivalence is not progress": Connes' trace formula reformulates RH as Weil positivity of a distribution, and the positivity is exactly as hard as RH — the 2020–2024 Connes–Consani–Moscovici results (archimedean-place positivity, prolate-operator UV spectrum, semi-local wave operators) are the first real movement here in decades, and they are the most promising live thread, but the global step still requires the arithmetic analogue of the Riemann–Roch/Hodge-index inequality that powered Weil's function-field proof. Third, the missing-Frobenius criticism: Katz–Sarnak makes clear that in every solved case the operator came from a cohomology of a geometric object of which the zeta function was an invariant; for Spec Z that object (an "F_1-curve," a Deninger dynamical system, Connes' scaling site) is still conjectural. My honest read: the spectral picture is very likely *true* — the Riemann operator "exists" in whatever sense the missing cohomology exists — but the program converts RH into building a new geometry rather than finding a clever Hamiltonian, and the physics-side activity (models, PT-symmetry, experiments) is best understood as exploration and evidence-gathering, not as a shrinking distance to proof. If a proof emerges from this family, expect it to look like Connes–Consani–Moscovici positivity or a Weil cohomology for Spec Z, with the quantum-chaos corpus serving as its heuristic scaffolding.
