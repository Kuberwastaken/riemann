## 1. Surveys & expository material

**On the Number of Prime Numbers less than a Given Quantity** — Bernhard Riemann, 1859, Monatsberichte der Berliner Akademie (English translation by David R. Wilkins, 1998).
- Link: https://www.maths.tcd.ie/pub/HistMath/People/Riemann/Zeta/EZeta.pdf (Wilkins' free translation; Clay also hosts scans of the original manuscript at https://www.claymath.org/collections/riemanns-1859-manuscript/)
- Local file: `riemann-1859-wilkins-english.pdf` (6 pp.)
- The founding document: Riemann's only paper on number theory. In eight pages he analytically continues ζ(s), proves the functional equation via theta functions, introduces the ξ-function, sketches the product formula over zeros, gives the explicit formula linking zeros to the prime-counting function, and remarks — almost in passing — that "it is very probable" that all the (nontrivial) roots lie on the critical line, adding that after some fleeting attempts he set the search for a proof aside "as it appears unnecessary for the immediate objective of my investigation." Both the count of zeros to height T and the density statement he asserts were only proven decades later (von Mangoldt, and the on-line proportion much later still). Essential primary source; everything in the field descends from this paper.

**Problems of the Millennium: the Riemann Hypothesis (official Clay problem description)** — Enrico Bombieri, 2000, Clay Mathematics Institute.
- Link: https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/riemann.pdf (from https://www.claymath.org/millennium/riemann-hypothesis/)
- Local file: `bombieri-clay-official-problem.pdf` (10 pp.)
- The official statement of the $1M Millennium Prize problem. Bombieri states RH, sketches the history (Riemann, Hadamard/de la Vallée Poussin, Hardy, Selberg, Levinson, Conrey's 2/5), and surveys the main evidence: numerical verification, the proven function-field analogue (Weil, Deligne), and the Hilbert–Pólya spectral idea including Montgomery pair correlation and the Odlyzko computations matching GUE statistics. He emphasizes the far-reaching consequences of RH and GRH for prime distribution and states his view that the deepest supporting evidence is the function-field analogy. This is the canonical "what exactly is the problem" reference.

**Problems of the Millennium: The Riemann Hypothesis (2004)** — Peter Sarnak, 2004, Clay Mathematics Institute Annual Report.
- Links: mirror http://home.olemiss.edu/~mbmilino/Sarnak_RH.pdf ; original Clay path https://www.claymath.org/library/annual_report/ar2004/04report_sarnak.pdf
- Local file: `sarnak-clay-2004-rh.pdf` (9 pp.)
- Sarnak's status report written for Clay as a complement to Bombieri's official description. He stresses the Grand Riemann Hypothesis (all automorphic L-functions), reviews what is unconditionally known (zero-free regions, positive proportion on the line, GUE statistics), and discusses the approaches he takes seriously: the function-field/monodromy analogy (Weil, Deligne, Katz–Sarnak), the spectral/Hilbert–Pólya direction, and random matrix theory as structural evidence. Famous for its sober bottom line: no approach is close, RH will require ideas not currently available, but partial progress (families, zero-density, subconvexity) is genuine mathematics with genuine applications. A standard citation for "expert consensus circa 2004."

**The Riemann Hypothesis** — J. Brian Conrey, 2003, Notices of the AMS 50 (3), 341–353.
- Link: https://www.ams.org/notices/200303/fea-conrey-web.pdf
- Local file: `conrey-notices-ams-2003.pdf` (10 pp.)
- Probably the most-cited modern survey of RH. Conrey covers the evidence (10^13 zeros, 40% on the line), the main equivalents (Mertens-type bounds, Robin's criterion, Nyman–Beurling, Li's criterion), and the principal research programs: random matrix theory and the Keating–Snaith moment conjectures, the function-field analogy, and the spectral interpretation. Notable for its candid sociology: Conrey observes that the RH literature splits into camps that barely interact, and for his much-quoted line that "the Riemann Hypothesis remains one of the classical unsolved problems, and it is not clear that the current tools are adequate." Includes the famous plots of ζ on the critical line and Odlyzko's pair-correlation data.

**The Riemann Hypothesis (expository chapter)** — J. Brian Conrey, 2011, in "Expeditions in Mathematics" (MAA Spectrum, ed. Shubin et al.).
- Link: https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/q2.pdf (free copy on Conrey's AIM publication page)
- Local file: `conrey-2011-rh-expository-maa.pdf` (18 pp.)
- Conrey's later, gentler exposition (written around the 150th anniversary of Riemann's paper), aimed at a broad audience: primes, logarithms, the infinite series, the critical strip, plus anecdotes about the problem's history and culture. Valuable as a record of how the leading RH survey-writer frames the problem for non-specialists, and it complements his 2003 Notices piece rather than replacing it. Conrey also co-organized the Bristol 2018 "Perspectives" meeting (below), which functions as his de-facto state-of-the-art update; his AIM publications page (https://aimath.org/~kaur/publications/) has free copies of most of his moment/ratios-conjecture papers that underpin the RMT program.

**Zeroes of zeta functions and symmetry** — Nicholas M. Katz and Peter Sarnak, 1999, Bulletin of the AMS 36 (1), 1–26.
- Link: https://www.ams.org/journals/bull/1999-36-01/S0273-0979-99-00766-1/S0273-0979-99-00766-1.pdf
- Local file: `katz-sarnak-bams-1999.pdf` (26 pp.)
- The manifesto of the "symmetry/families" world-view. Katz–Sarnak explain how, for zeta functions of curves over finite fields, RH is a theorem (Weil/Deligne) and the zeros are spectra of Frobenius acting on cohomology; letting the curve vary, the zero statistics match the eigenvalue statistics of classical compact groups (unitary, symplectic, orthogonal), with the symmetry type determined by the monodromy of the family. They conjecture the same universality for families of number-field L-functions (low-lying zeros), tying Montgomery–Odlyzko GUE phenomena to a concrete geometric mechanism. This paper is the standard reference for why many experts believe a proof of RH should come with a (co)homological or spectral structure attached, and it seeded twenty-five years of "families of L-functions" research.

**An essay on the Riemann Hypothesis** — Alain Connes, 2015, in "Open Problems in Mathematics" (Springer, eds. Nash & Rassias); arXiv:1509.05576.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05576 (pdf: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.05576)
- Local file: `connes-2015-essay-on-rh.pdf`
- The best recent (2015+) insider survey of *strategies* rather than results. Connes organizes attacks on RH into three families: (a) analytic/classical, (b) the algebraic-geometric dream of transplanting Weil's function-field proof to Spec(Z) — including his own adele class space / noncommutative geometry program, where the Weil explicit formula becomes a trace formula and RH becomes a positivity statement, and the later Connes–Consani work on the "arithmetic site" and toposes, and (c) spectral realizations (Hilbert–Pólya). He is frank about the obstructions: what is missing is an analogue of the Frobenius and of the geometric fixed-point formula over the "field with one element." Peter Woit called it "a remarkable document of a sort that too rarely gets written" (see blog section). Read this to understand what a serious, decades-long assault on RH looks like from the inside.

**Lectures on the Riemann Zeta Function** — Henryk Iwaniec, 2014, AMS University Lecture Series vol. 62.
- Link (record): https://bookstore.ams.org/ulect-62 — **not freely available; not downloaded** (AMS commercial title).
- Canonical modern lecture notes by one of the great analytic number theorists: proof of the classical theory, zero-free regions, and notably Levinson's method and Conrey's refinement showing more than 40% of zeros on the critical line, done Iwaniec's way. Recorded here as a standard reference for the "critical-line proportion" technology (now pushed to 41.7% by Pratt–Robles–Zaharescu–Zeindler 2019). Iwaniec also called the 2024 Guth–Maynard theorem "sensational" in Quanta (below).

**Riemann's Zeta Function** — H. M. Edwards, 1974 (Academic Press; Dover reprint 2001). **Record only, no free legal PDF.**
- The canonical historical-mathematical companion to Riemann's paper: it reconstructs Riemann's actual arguments (including the Riemann–Siegel formula from the Nachlass, following Siegel 1932) and proves the classical theory in Riemann's own terms. Includes Wilkins-independent translation of the 1859 memoir as an appendix. Universally recommended first serious book on RH.

**The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-function** — E. C. Titchmarsh, 1951; 2nd ed. revised by D. R. Heath-Brown, 1986, Oxford. **Record only, no free legal PDF.**
- The professional's encyclopedia of unconditional zeta theory: approximate functional equations, moments, Ω-theorems, zero-density estimates, and a full chapter of "consequences of the Riemann hypothesis." Heath-Brown's end-notes update each chapter. Together with Ivić's "The Riemann Zeta-Function" (1985) this is where the analytic state-of-the-art lived before the modern survey era. (Also worth recording: Borwein–Choi–Rooney–Weirathmueller, "The Riemann Hypothesis: A Resource for the Afficionado and Virtuoso Alike," CMS/Springer 2008 — an anthology reprinting Riemann, Bombieri's Clay description, Conrey's Notices survey and other originals; copies circulate online, e.g. https://bbs.pku.edu.cn/attach/8d/89/8d895d221266d1cd/book.pdf.)

**New progress on the zeta function: From old conjectures to a major breakthrough** — Enrico Bombieri, 2019, PNAS 116 (23), 11085–11086 (commentary).
- Links: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1906804116 ; free full text via Europe PMC (PMC6561272), fetched from https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC6561272?pdf=render
- Local file: `bombieri-2019-pnas-commentary.pdf` (2 pp.)
- Bombieri's invited commentary on the Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier Jensen-polynomial paper (below). He traces the Jensen–Pólya lineage (Jensen 1913 letters, Pólya 1927), explains what hyperbolicity of Jensen polynomials means, and frames GORZ 2019 as genuine progress on an abandoned program while carefully *not* claiming it brings RH within reach. Useful as the most authoritative sympathetic reading of that paper — to be weighed against Farmer's critique (below).

**Jensen polynomials for the Riemann zeta function and other sequences** — Michael Griffin, Ken Ono, Larry Rolen, Don Zagier, 2019, PNAS 116 (23), 11103–11110; arXiv:1902.07321.
- Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07321 ; PNAS open version https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6561287/
- Local file: `griffin-ono-rolen-zagier-2019-jensen.pdf`
- Pólya (1927) showed RH is equivalent to all Jensen polynomials J^{d,n} of the ξ-function being hyperbolic (all-real zeros); this was known only for degrees d ≤ 3. GORZ prove hyperbolicity for all d ≤ 8 and, for each fixed d, for all sufficiently large shift n (density-1 subset), via an asymptotic formula showing the renormalized Jensen polynomials converge to Hermite polynomials — which they interpret as a derivative-aspect confirmation of the GUE random-matrix prediction. The media narrative ("abandoned approach revived") came from the Emory press release; the mathematical significance for RH itself is contested — see Farmer below and the blog/media section. The paper is short, real, and correct; the dispute is only about what it portends.

**Jensen polynomials are not a plausible route to proving the Riemann Hypothesis** — David W. Farmer, 2020 (v2 2022), Advances in Mathematics 411 (2022); arXiv:2008.07206.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07206
- Local file: `farmer-2020-jensen-not-plausible.pdf` (13 pp.)
- The critical companion piece, from the AIM director of programs. Exact abstract lines: "…we find there is no justification for the suggested connection to the Riemann Hypothesis, nor for the suggested connection to the conjectured random matrix statistics for zeros of L-functions. These considerations suggest that Jensen polynomials, as well as a large class of related polynomials, are not useful for attacking the Riemann Hypothesis." Farmer shows the GORZ asymptotics live in a regime already governed by classical zero-density/unconditional results (each fixed-degree statement is implied by long-known facts about zeros in the bulk), so the hard content of RH is untouched; he proposes general criteria for judging when an "RH equivalent" is actually useful. Standard reference for the anatomy of over-interpreted equivalences.

**The De Bruijn–Newman constant is non-negative** — Brad Rodgers and Terence Tao, 2018/2020, Forum of Mathematics, Pi 8 (2020); arXiv:1801.05914.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05914
- Local file: `rodgers-tao-2018-de-bruijn-newman.pdf` (61 pp.)
- The heat-flow deformation H_t of the ξ-function has all-real zeros iff t ≥ Λ (the de Bruijn–Newman constant); RH is exactly Λ ≤ 0. De Bruijn (1950) proved Λ ≤ 1/2, Newman (1976) conjectured Λ ≥ 0 with the famous gloss that "the Riemann hypothesis, if true, is only barely so." Rodgers–Tao prove Newman's conjecture: Λ ≥ 0. The proof is a "conspiracy is unstable" argument — if Λ < 0, the zeros at time 0 would have to be in perfect local-equilibrium arrangement, contradicting known statistical results about zeta zeros. Interpretation (Tao's): there is no room to prove RH by proving a *stronger*, more regular statement; the universe barely permits RH at best. Downstream: Polymath15 (2018–19) pushed the other side to Λ ≤ 0.22 (see blog section).

**New large value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials** — Larry Guth and James Maynard, 2024 (v2 2026), arXiv:2405.20552 (to appear, Annals-level venue).
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20552
- Local file: `guth-maynard-2024-large-values.pdf`
- The biggest unconditional zero-related advance in decades: the first improvement since Ingham 1940 on zero-density at σ = 3/4, from N(3/4,T) ≪ T^{3/5+o(1)} to ≪ T^{13/25+o(1)}, via new large-value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials (raising a phase matrix to the 6th power, refusing stationary-phase simplification, additive-energy case analysis). Consequence: prime number theorem in almost-all short intervals (x, x+x^θ) now for θ > 2/15 instead of θ > 1/6. Not a step toward proving RH itself — it limits how *badly* and how *often* RH can fail, not whether it can fail once (see Tao's extracts below for exactly this distinction). Iwaniec: "It is sensational." Fields-medal context: Maynard (Fields 2022 for small gaps/primes with missing digits/Duffin–Schaeffer) and Guth (harmonic analysis, decoupling) bringing Fourier-analytic technology into the classical heart of zeta theory.

**Disproof of the Mertens conjecture** — Andrew Odlyzko and Herman te Riele, 1985, J. reine angew. Math. 357, 138–160.
- Link: http://www.dtc.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/arch/mertens.disproof.pdf (Odlyzko's archive; DigiZeitschriften scan)
- Local file: `odlyzko-teriele-1985-mertens-disproof.pdf` (24 pp.)
- Included here as the coda to the Stieltjes story (below): Mertens' conjecture |M(x)| ≤ √x — a strengthening of what Stieltjes claimed in 1885 — would have implied RH; Odlyzko–te Riele disproved it using lattice basis reduction (LLL) applied to high-precision zeta zeros, showing limsup M(x)/√x > 1.06 (without exhibiting an explicit x). Object lesson: natural-looking strengthenings of RH tend to be false ("RH is barely true if true"), which is one structural reason naive attack routes fail.

**Perspectives on the Riemann Hypothesis** — conference, University of Bristol / Heilbronn Institute, 4–7 June 2018. **(Record; no PDF.)**
- Links: https://www.bristolmathsresearch.org/meeting/heilbronn-perspectives-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/ ; talk videos: https://aimath.org/videos/rh2018/ and https://aimath.org/videos/rh-2018-videos/ ; Clay page: https://www.claymath.org/events/perspectives-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/
- Fourth in a series of RH conferences (after Seattle 1996, Vienna, New York); ~180 participants; organizers Conrey, Keating, Sarnak, Wiles. The speaker list is a map of which programs the field takes seriously: Bombieri (classical/analytic), Connes (noncommutative geometry / adele class space), Conrey and Keating (random matrix theory and moments), Katz and Sawin (function fields, monodromy), Sarnak, Soundararajan and Radziwiłł (analytic methods, moments), Tao (pseudorandomness/analysis), Skinner and W. Zhang (automorphic/arithmetic, Gross–Zagier-type and Iwasawa directions), K. Ball (Nyman–Beurling/functional-analytic criteria), Garrett (spectral/automorphic interpretations), Patterson (history). Videos are freely viewable — the closest thing to a comprehensive "current state of approaches" survey for the late 2010s.

## 2. Claimed and failed proofs

### Atiyah 2018

**The Riemann Hypothesis (preprint)** — Michael Atiyah, September 2018, unpublished 5-page preprint.
- Link: https://aperiodical.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/2018-The_Riemann_Hypothesis.pdf (also archived at https://archive.org/details/2018-the-riemann-hypothesis)
- Local file: `atiyah-2018-riemann-hypothesis-preprint.pdf`

**The Fine Structure Constant (companion preprint)** — Michael Atiyah, 2018, unpublished, "submitted to Proceedings A of the Royal Society" (never appeared).
- Link: https://empslocal.ex.ac.uk/people/staff/mrwatkin/zeta/atiyah_FSC.pdf
- Local file: `atiyah-2018-fine-structure-constant.pdf`

The story: On 24 September 2018, Sir Michael Atiyah (89, Fields Medal 1966, Abel Prize 2004) announced at the Heidelberg Laureate Forum a "simple proof" of RH, presented in a 45-minute talk and a 5-page preprint. **Claimed mechanism:** a "Todd function" T(s), attributed to constructions in the fine-structure-constant paper ("a novel fusion of ideas of von Neumann and Hirzebruch… based on an infinite iteration of exponentials"). T is asserted to be "weakly analytic" — a weak limit of analytic functions that is *polynomial on every compact convex set*. Assuming a zero b of ζ inside the critical strip off the critical line, Atiyah sets F(s) = T(1 + ζ(s + b)) − 1 and derives a contradiction from formal composition properties of T, concluding no such b exists. The same T(π) is claimed to yield a mathematical derivation of the fine structure constant α ≈ 1/137 (as a limit he calls Ж, "Zhe").

**Why it failed:** (1) No non-constant function with the claimed Todd properties can exist — a function analytic on C that agrees with a polynomial on any open/convex set is globally that polynomial (identity theorem), so the "locally polynomial, globally exotic" object at the heart of the proof is self-contradictory as described, and no rigorous construction is ever given (the FSC paper defines it only through renormalization metaphors). (2) The contradiction argument uses no specific property of ζ beyond generic analyticity, so if valid it would prove far too much — e.g. it would equally "prove" the (false) RH-analogue for functions like the Davenport–Heilbronn function, which satisfies a functional equation yet has off-line zeros. (3) The physics side is not well-posed: α is a running coupling measured experimentally, not a mathematical constant, so a closed-form "derivation" is category error (Sean Carroll's point). The Royal Society paper never appeared; the Clay Institute's problem status was unchanged; most senior experts declined even to comment publicly out of respect (Science, below). Atiyah, who had made similar late-life claims (a 12-page Feit–Thompson proof, a claimed solution of the S^6 complex-structure problem), died in January 2019. Timothy Gowers (on Twitter at the time) and the Aperiodical's post-mortem were representative: the document asserts rather than proves; "many have concluded that no function with such properties can exist" (Aperiodical, "final thoughts," https://aperiodical.com/2018/09/atiyah-riemann-hypothesis-proof-final-thoughts/).

- New Scientist, Gilead Amit, Sep 2018: "Famed mathematician claims proof of 160-year-old Riemann hypothesis."

### de Branges (1985–present)

**Apology for the Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis** — Louis de Branges, ongoing (dated versions 2004→), self-published on his Purdue page.
- Link: https://www.math.purdue.edu/~branges/apology.pdf
- Local file: `debranges-apology.pdf` (partly autobiographical/historical defense of his program — "apology" in the sense of apologia).

**The Riemann Hypothesis (current claimed proof)** — Louis de Branges, version dated 2017, 89 pp., self-published.
- Link: https://www.math.purdue.edu/~branges/proof-riemann-2017-04.pdf (from his homepage https://www.math.purdue.edu/~branges/)
- Local file: `debranges-proof-riemann-2017.pdf`

**A note on some positivity conditions related to zeta and L-functions** — J. Brian Conrey and Xian-Jin Li, 1998, IMRN 2000(18); arXiv:math/9812166.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9812166
- Local file: `conrey-li-1998-debranges-refutation.pdf` (10 pp.)

The story: de Branges is a serious mathematician — he proved the Bieberbach conjecture in 1984, a proof initially met with the same skepticism his RH claims receive, which was then verified (in Leningrad) and stood. Since the mid-1980s he has repeatedly claimed proofs of RH within his theory of Hilbert spaces of entire functions. **Claimed mechanism:** RH (and GRH for Dirichlet and quaternionic zeta functions, in later versions) would follow if certain positivity conditions hold for reproducing kernels / operators in de Branges spaces associated with the zeta function. **Why it fails:** Conrey–Li (1998) showed *numerically and provably, using known zeros*, that the required positivity conditions are violated for the Riemann ζ and for Dirichlet L-functions — the machine is fine as Hilbert-space theory, but its hypotheses are false for the actual zeta function, so the route as stated cannot prove RH. In June 2004 Purdue's press office nevertheless announced a claimed proof (press release: https://www.purdue.edu/uns/html4ever/2004/040608.DeBranges.Riemann.html), generating wide coverage (and criticism of Purdue for issuing a press release for an unrefereed 124-page PDF); Karl Sabbagh, who had profiled de Branges sympathetically in his 2003 book "The Riemann Hypothesis," published the "apology" documents. De Branges has continued revising (current version restricts to zeta functions "constructed from a discrete vector space… over the skew-field of quaternions"); no version has been accepted by a journal, and no expert has endorsed one. Contemporary blog record: Woit, "de Branges and the Riemann Hypothesis" (https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=35, June 2004) and "Stalking the Riemann Hypothesis" (https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/wordpress/?p=197).

### Historical near-misses and echoes

**Stieltjes, 1885.** Thomas Stieltjes announced — in Letter 71 to Hermite, communicated to the Comptes Rendus — that he had proved M(n)/√n is bounded (M = Mertens function), which would imply RH. He never produced the proof; nothing was found in his papers after his death (1894), and the claim was long treated as an open promissory note (Hadamard even cited it in his 1896 PNT paper as a reason his own weaker results were still worth publishing). Modern verdict: he was surely mistaken — Odlyzko–te Riele (1985, local file above) disproved the stronger Mertens conjecture |M(x)| ≤ √x, and boundedness of M(x)/√x is universally believed false (limsup is expected to be +∞), though strictly that disproof is still open. Primary-source study: "T. J. Stieltjes' letters concerning the zeta function," arXiv:1410.8592.

**Rademacher, 1943–45.** Hans Rademacher submitted a *disproof* of RH to Transactions of the AMS; it was accepted and scheduled for the May 1945 issue, then withdrawn at the last moment after Carl Ludwig Siegel found the flaw (accounts point to an error involving the multivaluedness of the complex logarithm). Time magazine ran the story on 30 April 1945 under "Science: As You Were," with Riemann's portrait captioned "Few understand it, none has proved it" (https://time.com/archive/6599805/science-as-you-were/; MacTutor account: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Extras/Riemann_Hypothesis_Time/). The canonical example of peer review + expert scrutiny working as intended, and of press interest in RH long predating the internet. (Good survey of this and other episodes: Richard Brent et al., "Some instructive mathematical errors," arXiv:2106.07269.)

**Opeyemi Enoch, 2015.** A lecturer at Federal University Oye-Ekiti, Nigeria, claimed a proof and was uncritically reported by BBC, Telegraph and Daily Mail as having won the Clay $1M; Clay simply pointed to its list of unsolved problems, and the "paper" turned out to be plagiarized nearly line-for-line from a 2013 paper by Werner Raab (coverage: https://qz.com/africa/552327/, Aperiodical: https://aperiodical.com/2015/11/riemann-hypothesis-not-proved/). Pure media failure; no mathematical content.

**The arXiv/viXra phenomenon.** RH attracts a continuous stream of claimed proofs and disproofs — dozens per year on arXiv's math.GM (the "general mathematics" category where moderators reroute such submissions) and on viXra, plus predatory-journal "publications" (search incidentally surfaced e.g. arXiv:1305.6845 "The Riemann hypothesis proved," arXiv:1402.5952, arXiv:2005.12525, a "Purely Mathematical Proof" in a pay-to-publish venue, a Cambridge Engage preprint, etc.). The pattern is stable: elementary manipulations of the Euler product or functional equation valid only in Re(s) > 1, illegitimate term-by-term rearrangements of conditionally convergent series, or "physics" arguments assuming an unconstructed self-adjoint operator. None engage the known structural obstructions (see synthesis). Per standing instructions these are noted as a phenomenon, not cataloged.

## 3. Blog and media coverage — key extracts

### Terence Tao (What's New + Mathstodon)

**"The De Bruijn–Newman constant is non-negative"** — 19 Jan 2018, https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2018/01/19/the-de-bruijn-newman-constant-is-non-negativ/ (note the truncated slug "negativ" — the canonical URL really is misspelled).
> Announces the Rodgers–Tao proof of Newman's conjecture Λ ≥ 0. Newman's original gloss, quoted approvingly throughout this literature: the conjecture expresses that "the Riemann hypothesis, if true, is only barely so." Mechanism extract: under backwards heat flow, zeros of H_t repel and settle toward arithmetic-progression equilibrium; if Λ < 0, the zeta zeros at t = 0 would already have to be in that rigid equilibrium, contradicting known results on zero statistics (pair correlation on average). So RH cannot be proven "with room to spare."

**Polymath15** — proposal 24 Jan 2018, https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2018/01/24/polymath-proposal-upper-bounding-the-de-bruijn-newman-constant/; eleven working threads through 2019 (e.g. https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2018/05/04/polymath15-ninth-thread-going-below-0-22/); wiki: https://michaelnielsen.org/polymath/index.php?title=De_Bruijn-Newman_constant

## 4. Synthesis

**What the expert-consensus surveys agree on.** Across Bombieri (2000), Sarnak (2004), Conrey (2003/2011), Katz–Sarnak (1999), Connes (2015), and the Bristol 2018 program, the consensus is remarkably stable. (1) The evidence for RH is overwhelming but structural, not merely numerical: the proven function-field analogue (Weil/Deligne) and the quantitative match between zeta-zero statistics and random-matrix (GUE) predictions (Montgomery, Odlyzko, Keating–Snaith, and now Arguin–Bourgade–Radziwiłł) convince essentially everyone that RH is true *and* that its truth has a spectral/geometric mechanism behind it — this is the Hilbert–Pólya intuition in modern dress. (2) The approaches taken seriously are exactly the ones represented at Bristol: the algebraic-geometric program of transplanting Weil's proof to Spec(Z) (Connes' adele class space and F1-geometry being its most developed form); spectral realizations of the zeros; random matrix theory as a source of precise conjectures (moments, ratios) that any real proof should explain; positivity criteria (Weil's explicit-formula positivity, Li's criterion, Nyman–Beurling) as reformulations awaiting a mechanism; and hard unconditional analysis (zero-free regions, zero-density, critical-line proportions) as the only game that provably moves. (3) Everyone agrees no current approach is close: each program has an identified missing ingredient (no Frobenius over Z, no self-adjoint operator actually constructed, no way to make positivity hold), and the honest formulations — Sarnak's 2004 assessment, Conrey's "not clear that the current tools are adequate," Tao's 2024 "still very far from fully resolving" — have not changed in twenty years even as genuinely major partial results (Rodgers–Tao, Guth–Maynard) arrived. Notably, the two real breakthroughs of the past decade both *constrain the failure modes* of RH (Λ ≥ 0: RH is at best barely true; Guth–Maynard: violations, if any, are sparse) rather than advance a proof of it.

**The common anatomy of failed proofs.** The failures documented above, spanning 140 years and every level of mathematical seriousness, share a recognizable anatomy. First, the claimed mechanism is always a general-purpose machine that never engages the specific arithmetic of ζ: Atiyah's Todd-function argument used nothing about zeta and would have "proved" the same for functions (Davenport–Heilbronn) known to violate the conclusion; de Branges' Hilbert-space positivity is sound abstract theory whose hypotheses Conrey–Li showed are simply false for the actual zeta function; countless arXiv/viXra attempts manipulate the Euler product where it doesn't converge. A valid proof must somewhere exploit what makes ζ special, and must fail for the known counterexample-functions — a filter almost no claimed proof passes. Second, the strengthening trap: it is tempting to prove RH by proving something cleaner and stronger, but the strong statements tend to be false — Mertens' conjecture (Stieltjes' route) was disproven; Λ < 0 (RH "with room to spare") is now a theorem-level impossibility. RH sits at the exact boundary of truth, so any proof must be razor-thin, which is precisely Tao's point about why partial statistical progress cannot be pushed to the full statement: everything known excludes *many* bad zeros, nothing known excludes *one*. Third, the sociology is diagnostic: real progress (Rodgers–Tao, Guth–Maynard) arrives quietly as long technical preprints absorbed by the community within days, while failed claims arrive as announcements — press releases (Purdue 2004, Emory's overstated 2019 framing), lectures to general audiences (Heidelberg 2018), or media stories (Enoch 2015, Time 1945) — with the technical document either absent, unverifiable, or refuted by an already-published obstruction. The rational Bayesian stance the field has converged on: any new claim must first say why it evades the known no-go facts (Conrey–Li positivity failure, Davenport–Heilbronn universality test, Λ ≥ 0, the one-bad-zero obstruction); none of the famous failures ever did.

## 5. Addendum (2026-08-11): wessorh/hprime — a computational Hilbert–Pólya search, honestly self-retracted

- **What:** github.com/wessorh/hprime (Rick Wesson; active through Aug 2026). Local files: `papers/surveys-expository/Wesson-2026-hprime-strong-resolvent.pdf` (broken font encoding; unreadable without OCR) and `.tex` (source, readable). Ingested at the direction of the project owner as "slightly relevant".
- **The claim chain:** a tridiagonal "hybrid Hilbert plane operator" H_N with diagonal V(z) = γ_z — **the z-th zeta ordinate itself** (Riemann–von Mangoldt approximation + Newton refinement for z > 64) — and couplings (1/N)√(V(z)V(z+1)); the strong-resolvent paper proves H_N → H_∞ self-adjoint with eigenvalues = lim λ_k^(N), and concludes that if μ_∞ = μ_ζ then "the zeta zeros are eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator", hence RH.
- **Adjudication (independent of the repo's own retraction):** circular at the root — the zeros are the *input data* of the operator, so the spectrum tracking the zeros is Gershgorin bookkeeping (off-diagonals are O(γ/N)), and reality of the spectrum is a property of any real symmetric matrix, i.e. of the *choice* to feed in real numbers γ_z. Hilbert–Pólya requires an operator canonically attached to ζ whose spectrum is *provably* the zeros; here the attachment *is* the zero list, and the final step ("functional-equation symmetry then forces Re s = 1/2") is a non sequitur. The convergence analysis is also unsound on its own terms (the doubling comparison moves diagonal entries by γ_{2z} − γ_z ≍ z/log z, and the claimed O(1/(N log N)) cancellation is asserted, not proven — the TeX even retains a mid-proof "Wait—this gives a LARGE shift" self-correction, a visible LLM-drafting artifact). None of this matters to the conclusion, which is empty by circularity; the one correct and honest sentence is the last: "the remaining gap is proving μ_∞ = μ_ζ" — which *is* the Hilbert–Pólya problem.
- **Credit where due:** the repo's own README (status Aug 2026) retracts all three of its approaches with correct diagnoses (Gershgorin circularity — caught by xAI's Grok; correlation degrading with more primes = small-N artifact; bounded Cayley-graph spectrum + spurious Lanczos Ritz values). As a public record of failure modes it has genuine pedagogical value, and it exemplifies rows of this archive's no-go map: eigenvalue-matching without mechanism, and Berry–Keating's "mean density is cheap" (any operator with the right counting function matches the zeros to leading order).
- **Filed under:** failed proofs (§2 lineage), as a 2026 AI-era specimen: honest process, wrong construction, self-corrected in public.
