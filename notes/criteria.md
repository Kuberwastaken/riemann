# Equivalent Criteria & Reformulations of the Riemann Hypothesis

Research notes, July 2026. Local PDFs live in `/root/dev/Riemann/papers/criteria/`.
Scope: every equivalence that has been seriously proposed as an attack route, plus the de Branges saga.

## 1. Weil's positivity criterion

**Name:** Weil's explicit-formula positivity criterion.
**Authors/year:** André Weil, "Sur les 'formules explicites' de la théorie des nombres premiers", Comm. Sém. Math. Univ. Lund (Medd. Lunds Univ. Mat. Sem.) 1952, tome supplémentaire, 252–265. (No free scan located; reprinted in Weil's Œuvres Scientifiques II.)
**Modern exposition:** E. Bombieri, "Remarks on Weil's quadratic functional in the theory of prime numbers, I", Rend. Mat. Acc. Lincei (9) 11 (2000), 183–233 — free scan: http://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2000_9_11_3_183_0
**Local files:** `Bombieri-2000-Weil-quadratic-functional.pdf`; recent: `Connes-Consani-2020-Weil-positivity.pdf` (arXiv:2006.13771), `Groskin-2026-truncated-Weil-form.pdf` (arXiv:2607.02828).
**Statement:** RH holds iff the Weil quadratic functional built from the explicit formula is positive semidefinite: for every smooth compactly supported f on (0,∞), W(f∗f̃) ≥ 0, where W(g) = Σ_ρ ĝ(ρ) is the sum of the Mellin transform over nontrivial zeros, expressed on the "arithmetic side" as an archimedean term minus a sum over prime powers. Equivalently, Σ_ρ ĥ(ρ) ≥ 0 for all h = f∗f̃.
**Attack attempts:** This is the reformulation closest to the Grothendieck–Deligne proof of RH over function fields, where the analogous positivity is the Castelnuovo/Hodge index inequality on the surface C×C — hence four decades of attempts to build an "arithmetic surface" Spec Z ×_{F1} Spec Z on which a Hodge-index argument would run (Connes' adele class space, Deninger's foliated dynamical systems, Connes–Consani's F1-geometry and scaling site). Bombieri (2000) made the functional analytically concrete: the functional attains a minimum on the unit ball of L²(I) for a fixed interval I, and (Yoshida's theorem, reproved there) positivity does hold for test functions supported in sufficiently short intervals around 1. Connes–Consani (2020) gave a conceptual "reason" for positivity at the archimedean place via the semi-local trace formula, Sonin spaces, and prolate spheroidal functions, controlling the discrepancy with Toeplitz-matrix theory. The 2026 Groskin note quantifies the archimedean tail of the truncated quadratic form. Nobody has extended the positivity to test functions of unbounded support, which is what RH requires.
**Status:** Open; positivity proved only for restricted (short-support) test-function classes and, conceptually, at the archimedean place.
**Assessment:** The gold-standard reformulation — it is the *only* one that provably worked in a parallel universe (function fields). But it is a pure relocation: the explicit formula is an identity, and "positivity for all f" repackages the same global information as the zeros themselves. The difficulty reappears as the lack of an arithmetic replacement for the algebraic surface and its intersection theory.

## 2. Li's criterion + Keiper–Li coefficients

**Name:** Li's criterion (non-negativity of λ_n).
**Authors/year:** Xian-Jin Li, "The positivity of a sequence of numbers and the Riemann hypothesis", J. Number Theory 65 (1997), 325–333. No free PDF found (ScienceDirect: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X97921375). Precursor: J. B. Keiper, "Power series expansions of Riemann's ξ function", Math. Comp. 58 (1992), 765–773 (free at AMS: https://www.ams.org/journals/mcom/1992-58-198/S0025-5718-1992-1122072-5/ — Cloudflare blocked curl, not stored locally).
**Companion:** E. Bombieri & J. C. Lagarias, "Complements to Li's criterion for the Riemann hypothesis", J. Number Theory 77 (1999), 274–287. No free PDF (open-archive page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X99923922); content covered by `Sekatskii-2013-generalized-Bombieri-Lagarias.pdf` (arXiv:1304.7895).
**Local files:** `Voros-2004-sharpening-Li.pdf` (math/0404213), `Coffey-2005-Li-criterion-verification.pdf` (math-ph/0505052), `Maslanka-2004-Li-coefficients.pdf` (math/0402168), `Voros-2016-KeiperLi-simplified.pdf` (1602.03292), `Lagarias-2004-Li-automorphic.pdf` (math/0404394), `Sekatskii-2013-generalized-Bombieri-Lagarias.pdf`.
**Statement:** With λ_n = Σ_ρ [1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n] = (1/(n−1)!) d^n/ds^n [s^{n−1} log ξ(s)]|_{s=1}, RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n ≥ 1. (Keiper had introduced λ_n/n in 1992 and observed their positivity numerically.)
**Attack attempts:** Bombieri–Lagarias showed the criterion is a completely general fact about multisets of complex numbers (positivity of Σ_ρ Re[1−(1−1/ρ)^{−n}]-type sums ⟺ all points in Re s ≤ 1/2), and gave an arithmetic formula for λ_n via the Guinand–Weil explicit formula, tying Li positivity to Weil positivity for the specific test functions (1−(1−1/ρ)^n have kernel interpretation). Keiper (1992), Maślanka (3300 coefficients, 2004), Coffey (2005, bounds via the η_j coefficients of ζ'/ζ) and others computed λ_n: they split into a smooth trend n(log n)/2 + (γ−1−log 2π)n/2 plus tiny oscillations. Voros (2004, 2016) proved the sharp dichotomy: under RH λ_n grows like the trend; if RH fails, λ_n oscillates with exponentially growing amplitude. Arias de Reyna, "Asymptotics of Keiper–Li coefficients", Funct. Approx. Comment. Math. 45 (2011), 7–21 (https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.facm/1317045228, not freely downloadable) sharpened this: RH ⟺ the deviations y_m = λ_m − trend form an ℓ² sequence — so numerics that only see the trend are structurally *incapable* of giving evidence beyond the height to which zeros are already verified. Lagarias extended λ_n ≥ 0 to automorphic L-functions.
**Status:** Open. All computed λ_n positive; known to be implied by (and to imply) nothing weaker than RH itself; positivity proved for n up to ranges corresponding to verified zeros.
**Assessment:** Pure relocation with a seductive surface: a countable list of concrete real numbers to check. The difficulty reappears exactly in the oscillatory part of λ_n, which encodes hypothetical off-line zeros; each λ_n is a zero-sum, so certifying λ_n ≥ 0 for all n is as hard as RH. Its real value has been conceptual (bridge between Weil positivity and concrete sequences) and diagnostic (Arias de Reyna's accuracy theorem killing naive numerical programs).

## 3. Nyman–Beurling criterion and the Báez-Duarte strengthening

**Name:** Nyman–Beurling closure criterion.
**Authors/year:** B. Nyman, PhD thesis "On the one-dimensional translation group and semi-group in certain function spaces", Uppsala 1950 (no scan located); A. Beurling, "A closure problem related to the Riemann zeta-function", PNAS 41 (1955), 312–314.
**Local files:** `Beurling-1955-closure-problem.pdf` (via Europe PMC, PMC528084), `Baez-Duarte-2002-strengthened-NB.pdf` (arXiv:math/0202141), `Baez-Duarte-2002-strengthened-NB-2.pdf` (math/0205003), `Baez-Duarte-2005-Moebius-convolutions.pdf` (math/0504402), `Burnol-2001-NB-lower-bound.pdf` (math/0103058), `Burnol-2002-Baez-Duarte-theorem.pdf` (math/0202166), `Bettin-Conrey-Farmer-2012-NB-optimal.pdf` (1211.5191), `Darses-2020-generalized-NB-polynomial.pdf` (2006.02953), `Derevyanko-Kovalenko-2018-NB-cotangent-sums.pdf` (1811.04399), `Ehm-2024-NB-Gram-matrices.pdf` (2405.06349).
**Statement:** Let ρ(x) = {x} and ρ_a(x) = {a/x} (dilations of the fractional part). Nyman–Beurling: RH ⟺ the characteristic function χ_{(0,1)} lies in the L²(0,∞)-closure of the span of {ρ_θ : 0 < θ ≤ 1} restricted appropriately. (Beurling: ζ has no zeros with Re s > 1/p iff the analogous closure holds in L^p(0,1).) Báez-Duarte (2002): one may restrict to *integer* dilations — RH ⟺ χ_{(0,1)} ∈ closure of span{ρ_n : n = 1, 2, 3, …} — turning RH into: lim_{N→∞} d_N = 0, where d_N² = inf over Dirichlet-polynomial coefficients of the distance from χ to span{ρ_1,…,ρ_N}.
**Attack attempts:** This became the most actively *quantified* equivalence. Báez-Duarte–Balazard–Landreau–Saias ("Notes sur la fonction ζ de Riemann, 3", Adv. Math. 149 (2000)) proved unconditionally d_N² ≥ (Σ_{ρ on line} 1/|ρ|²)(1+o(1))/log N; Burnol (math/0103058, Adv. Math. 2002) improved the constant to Σ m(ρ)²/|ρ|² (multiplicities) and conjectured with Báez-Duarte that d_N² ~ (2+γ−log 4π)/log N, i.e. the zeros on the line already saturate the distance. Bettin–Conrey–Farmer (1211.5191) proved the conjectured asymptotic *assuming* RH plus a bound Σ_{|Im ρ|<T} 1/|ζ'(ρ)|² ≪ T^{3/2−δ} — so even computing the distance asymptotics is RH-hard. The Vasyunin-sum/cotangent-sum route (Bettin–Conrey period functions; survey in 1811.04399) reduced the Gram matrix ⟨ρ_m,ρ_n⟩ to explicit cotangent sums V(m/n) whose distributional theory (Maier–Rassias et al.) is now a small industry; Ehm (2405.06349) and Darses–Hillion (2006.02953, polynomial approximations in a generalized NB criterion) continue structural work. Báez-Duarte's "Möbius convolutions" note (math/0504402) recasts NB in real-variable Möbius-summation terms.
**Status:** Open. Unconditional: d_N² ≫ 1/log N (so convergence to 0, if true, is as slow as possible); conditional asymptotics known. Natural approximants (e.g. coefficients μ(n)(1−log n/log N)) get within the conjectured constant but cannot beat 1/log N.
**Why it stalls:** The functional-analytic packaging is exact but the minimization problem's Gram matrix is built from ζ on the critical line; the lower bound shows the on-line zeros themselves obstruct fast convergence, and to show d_N → 0 at all one must rule out off-line zeros — the same tautology. Positivity/orthogonality difficulty reappears as the invertibility/conditioning of the Gram matrix of {ρ_n}, equivalently the non-vanishing of ζ.
**Assessment:** Relocation, but the most honest one: it converted RH into a concrete numerical variational problem and produced genuinely new unconditional theorems (the 1/log N barrier) that *explain* why the approach cannot succeed incrementally.

## 4. Robin's criterion, Lagarias' criterion, Nicolas' criterion (arithmetic/elementary family)

**Name:** Robin's criterion.
**Authors/year:** G. Robin, "Grandes valeurs de la fonction somme des diviseurs et hypothèse de Riemann", J. Math. Pures Appl. 63 (1984), 187–213. (No free scan located.)
**Local files:** `Lagarias-2000-elementary-harmonic.pdf` (arXiv:math/0008177), `Choie-2006-Robin-criterion.pdf` (math/0604314, publ. J. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux 19 (2007), free also at https://www.numdam.org/article/JTNB_2007__19_2_357_0.pdf), `Morrill-Platt-2018-Robin-20free.pdf` (1809.10813, INTEGERS 21 (2021) #A28), `Axler-2021-Robin-inequality.pdf` (2110.13478), `Nicolas-2012-Euler-function-RH.pdf` (1202.0729), `Choie-Planat-Sole-2010-Nicolas-criterion.pdf` (1012.3613).
**Statements:**
- Robin: RH ⟺ σ(n) < e^γ n log log n for all n > 5040.
- Lagarias (An elementary problem equivalent to RH, Amer. Math. Monthly 109 (2002), 534–543): RH ⟺ σ(n) ≤ H_n + e^{H_n} log H_n for all n ≥ 1, H_n the n-th harmonic number — no exceptional set, statable to a first-year student.
- Nicolas ("Petites valeurs de la fonction d'Euler", J. Number Theory 17 (1983); refined 2012): RH ⟺ N_k/φ(N_k) > e^γ log log N_k for all primorials N_k; and under RH the normalized quantity c(N_k) converges, while if RH fails it oscillates unboundedly.
**Attack attempts:** The strategy is to prove Robin's inequality on ever-larger classes of integers, hoping to corner the hypothetical minimal counterexample ("superabundant" territory). Choie–Lichiardopol–Moree–Solé (2007): any counterexample n > 5040 must be even, not squarefree, not squarefull, and divisible by a fifth power > 1 — so RH ⟺ Robin holds for all 5-full-free... in particular Robin holds for all odd n and all squarefree n. Morrill–Platt extended "t-free" verification from 5-, 7-, 11-free to 20-free integers, and computations (Briggs; Platt) verify Robin's inequality for 5040 < n ≤ 10^{10^{13}}. Axler (2021) sharpened effective bounds giving further classes. On the Nicolas side, Nicolas (2012) proved the sharp dichotomy for c(N_k) (limit e^γ(2+β−log 4π) under RH, oscillation otherwise); Choie–Planat–Solé related Nicolas' criterion to Chebyshev-function inequalities. Any counterexample to either inequality is known to be astronomically large and colossally abundant, so no numerical disproof route exists in practice.
**Status:** Open; verified for enormous ranges and many arithmetic classes; the residual class (even, 5-full, colossally abundant, > 10^{10^{13}}) is exactly where the extreme values of σ(n)/n live.
**Assessment:** Relocation into extremal arithmetic: σ(n)/n is large precisely on primorial-like integers, and its extreme size is controlled by Mertens-type products over primes, i.e. by Chebyshev's θ(x) − x, i.e. by the error term in the PNT — which is RH again. The elementary appearance is real (Lagarias' form needs no analysis to state) and pedagogically valuable, but every proof attempt immediately re-derives the need for square-root cancellation in θ(x). Its genuine product: sharp effective prime-counting inequalities (Rosser–Schoenfeld tradition, Axler) of independent use.

## 5. Balazard–Saias–Yor integral criterion and Volchkov's equality

**Name:** BSY criterion; Volchkov's criterion.
**Authors/year:** M. Balazard, E. Saias, M. Yor, "Notes sur la fonction ζ de Riemann, 2", Adv. Math. 143 (1999), 284–287 (no free PDF located; Elsevier page doi:10.1006/aima.1998.1797). V. V. Volchkov, "On an equality equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis", Ukrain. Math. J. 47 (1995), 422–423 (Springer https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01056314).
**Local files:** `Sekatskii-2008-log-zeta-integral-equalities.pdf` (arXiv:0806.1596 — Sekatskii–Beltraminelli–Merlini, proves a family of contour-integral equalities containing BSY and Volchkov as special cases).
**Statements:**
- BSY: RH ⟺ ∫_{−∞}^{∞} log|ζ(1/2+it)| / (1/4+t²) dt = 0. (The integral always equals 2π Σ_{Re ρ>1/2} log|ρ/(1−ρ)| ≥ 0, so RH ⟺ vanishing.)
- Volchkov: RH ⟺ ∫_0^∞ (1−12t²)(1+4t²)^{−3} ∫_{1/2}^∞ log|ζ(σ+it)| dσ dt = π(3−γ)/32.
**Attack attempts:** BSY was proved both by Hadamard-factorization and, strikingly, by Yor's probabilistic reading (expectation of log|ζ| along a Cauchy-distributed point of the critical line). The route it suggests — show one weighted mean of log|ζ| on the line vanishes — has attracted work on evaluating/bounding the integral: unconditional computations show the integral is extremely small (numerically ≤ 10^{−9} range in truncations), and Sekatskii–Beltraminelli–Merlini generalized to a whole family of equalities equivalent to RH (also for arg ζ: ∫ 2t·arg ζ(1/2+it)/(1/4+t²)² dt = π(γ−3)), hoping some member would be provable. Balazard's school also connected it to the "strong" density/completeness problems (cf. the annotated bibliography, Millennial Conference 2002). None of the equalities has been proved.
**Status:** Open. The integrals are provably ≥ 0 (or = target constant) with equality iff RH; numerics consistent with 0 to high precision.
**Assessment:** Elegant relocation: positivity appears with total transparency — the integral *is* Σ_{off-line ρ} log|ρ/(1−ρ)|, a sum of manifestly positive penalties for off-line zeros. Proving it vanishes requires exactly ruling out those zeros; the weight 1/(1/4+t²) buys nothing asymptotically. Useful mainly as the cleanest "one-line" analytic form of RH and as a numerical diagnostic.

## 6. Riesz criterion, Hardy–Littlewood series criterion, Báez-Duarte's sequential version

**Name:** Riesz (1916) and Hardy–Littlewood (1918) power-series criteria; Báez-Duarte's discrete Riesz-like criterion (2005).
**Authors/year:** M. Riesz, "Sur l'hypothèse de Riemann", Acta Math. 40 (1916), 185–190; G. H. Hardy & J. E. Littlewood, "Contributions to the theory of the Riemann zeta-function and the theory of the distribution of primes", Acta Math. 41 (1918), 119–196; L. Báez-Duarte, "A sequential Riesz-like criterion for the Riemann hypothesis", Int. J. Math. Math. Sci. 2005:21, 3527–3537.
**Local files:** `Riesz-1916-translation.pdf` (English translation, from M. Wolf's rare-papers page), `Hardy-Littlewood-1918-contributions.pdf` (same source), `BaezDuarte-2005-sequential-Riesz.pdf` (via Semantic Scholar), `Cislo-Wolf-2006-Riesz-BaezDuarte-equivalence.pdf` (math/0607782), `Cislo-Wolf-2008-Riesz-BaezDuarte.pdf` (0807.2971), `Wolf-2006-BaezDuarte-evidence.pdf` (math/0605485), `Maslanka-2006-BaezDuarte-Rice-integrals.pdf` (math/0603713), `Paris-2021-Riesz-function-numerical.pdf` (2107.02800), `Garg-Maji-2022-HL-Riesz-GRH.pdf` (2208.07596).
**Statements:**
- Riesz: RH ⟺ R(x) := Σ_{k≥1} (−1)^{k+1} x^k / ((k−1)! ζ(2k)) = O(x^{1/4+ε}).
- Hardy–Littlewood: RH ⟺ Σ_{k≥1} (−x)^k / (k! ζ(2k+1)) = O(x^{−1/4+ε}) as x → ∞.
- Báez-Duarte: with c_k = Σ_{j=0}^k (−1)^j C(k,j) / ζ(2j+2), RH ⟺ c_k = O(k^{−3/4+ε}); moreover c_k = O(k^{−3/4}) would give simplicity of the zeros. (Conjecturally c_k ~ k^{−3/4} oscillating with amplitude set by the first zero.)
**Attack attempts:** Dormant for ~90 years because R(x) is brutally hard to compute (catastrophic cancellation). Báez-Duarte's sequential form made it computable: Maślanka (Rice-integral representation), Wolf (math/0605485) computed c_k up to k ~ 10^5 and saw the predicted k^{−3/4} oscillations driven by the first Gram-point frequencies; Cisło–Wolf unified Riesz and Báez-Duarte as a two-parameter family of criteria and studied which exponents are reachable. Paris (2021) gave rigorous asymptotic machinery for evaluating R(x); Garg–Maji (2022, Monatsh. Math.) generalized the Riesz/HL criteria to a one-parameter family for Dirichlet L-functions (GRH), one of the few genuinely new equivalence theorems of the 2020s.
**Status:** Open. Numerics beautifully consistent with RH + simple zeros; no unconditional bound anywhere near the required exponent (best trivial bounds give exponent 1/2 for c_k, and 3/4 is precisely the RH threshold... the gap between 1/2 and 3/4 *is* RH).
**Assessment:** Relocation into asymptotics of alternating sums whose terms involve 1/ζ at integers: the required square-root-cancellation reappears as the size of a Möbius-weighted sum (R(x) = Σ μ(n)/n² · f(x/n²)-type), i.e. it is the M(x) = O(x^{1/2+ε}) problem wearing a smooth weight. Value: gave the first RH-equivalent sequences cheap enough for serious numerics.

## 7. Redheffer matrix determinant equivalence

**Name:** Redheffer determinant criterion.
**Authors/year:** R. Redheffer, "Eine explizit lösbare Optimierungsaufgabe", Int. Schriftenreihe Numer. Math. 36 (1977), 213–216 (no free scan). Key studies: Barrett–Forcade–Pollington, Linear Alg. Appl. 1988; R. C. Vaughan, "On the eigenvalues of Redheffer's matrix" I (1993), II (1996).
**Local files:** `Redheffer-2025-largest-singular-vector.pdf` (arXiv:2502.09489, Clément–Steinerberger).
**Statement:** Let A_n be the 0-1 matrix with a_{ij} = 1 iff j = 1 or i | j. Then det A_n = M(n) (the Mertens function), hence RH ⟺ det A_n = O(n^{1/2+ε}).
**Attack attempts:** The hope was that linear algebra / spectral theory would say something about a determinant that number theory cannot. Vaughan determined the spectrum's structure: n − ⌊log₂ n⌋ − 1 eigenvalues equal to 1, two dominant real eigenvalues ≈ ±√n, and the rest small (≪ (log n)^{2/5} unconditionally); but det A_n is the *product*, and the RH-critical information hides in the cluster of eigenvalues near 1 — bounding their product is exactly as hard as bounding M(n). Recent work (Clément–Steinerberger 2025) studies the largest singular vector, showing the dominant singular data is governed by the divisor structure, again orthogonal to the RH-critical directions. Graph-theoretic reformulations (counting path systems in the divisibility graph) have added combinatorial language but no bounds.
**Status:** Open; a curiosity that has produced good linear algebra and zero progress on M(n).
**Assessment:** Pure relocation, and a cautionary tale: the determinant identity is combinatorially trivial (Möbius inversion), so the spectral reformulation manufactures no new structure. The difficulty reappears as the near-unit eigenvalue cluster — a spectral disguise of Möbius cancellation.

## 8. Franel–Landau Farey-sequence equivalence

**Name:** Franel–Landau criterion.
**Authors/year:** J. Franel, "Les suites de Farey et le problème des nombres premiers"; E. Landau, "Bemerkungen zu der vorstehenden Abhandlung von Herrn Franel", both Nachr. Ges. Wiss. Göttingen Math.-Phys. Kl. 1924, 198–201 and 202–206. Free record: https://eudml.org/doc/59156 (GDZ scan; EUDML blocks bots, not stored locally).
**Local files:** `BCZ-2024-Farey-discretized-RH.pdf` (arXiv:2407.03099, from the Boca–Cobeli–Zaharescu map to a discretized analog of RH).
**Statement:** Let F_v = (r_1 < … < r_{Φ(v)}) be the Farey fractions of order v. RH ⟺ Σ_{j} |r_j − j/Φ(v)| = O(v^{1/2+ε}) (equivalently with Σ(r_j − j/Φ(v))² and exponent −1+ε): the Farey fractions must be equidistributed with square-root precision.
**Attack attempts:** The dream: prove equidistribution of Farey fractions by dynamical/geometric means (they arise from the horocycle flow on SL₂(Z)\SL₂(R)). Zagier and Sarnak observed that effective equidistribution of long closed horocycles at the RH-critical rate would give RH; Verjovsky, and later the Boca–Cobeli–Zaharescu (BCZ) map school, recast Farey statistics dynamically — the 2024 BCZ paper (local file) pushes a discretized analog. All known dynamical equidistribution rates fall short of the square-root barrier (they hit the same exponent wall as the classical error terms). Kanemitsu–Yoshimoto and others produced many variant Farey-sum equivalences.
**Status:** Open. Unconditional Farey discrepancy bounds correspond to the zero-free region, no better.
**Assessment:** Relocation into homogeneous dynamics — attractive because ergodic theory sometimes proves polynomial rates, but the needed rate is precisely square-root cancellation in Möbius/Mertens (Landau's paper already showed the equivalence to M(x)), and horocycle equidistribution rates are themselves governed by the spectral gap + the same arithmetic sums. The difficulty reappears as effective equidistribution constants.

## 9. Speiser's criterion

**Name:** Speiser's criterion (zeros of ζ′).
**Authors/year:** A. Speiser, "Geometrisches zur Riemannschen Zetafunktion", Math. Ann. 110 (1934), 514–521 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01448042, paywalled; no free scan found). Quantitative version: N. Levinson & H. L. Montgomery, "Zeros of the derivatives of the Riemann zeta-function", Acta Math. 133 (1974), 49–65.
**Local files:** `Garunkstis-2019-Speiser-Selberg-class.pdf` (arXiv:1904.03123; see also Garunkštis–Šimėnas, "On the Speiser equivalent for the Riemann hypothesis", Eur. J. Math. 1 (2015), 337–350).
**Statement:** RH ⟺ ζ′(s) has no zeros in the open left half of the critical strip 0 < Re s < 1/2. Levinson–Montgomery quantified: ζ and ζ′ have essentially the same number of zeros left of the line, so RH is also equivalent to "few" ζ′ zeros there.
**Attack attempts:** Speiser's geometric argument (curves Im ζ = 0) initiated the study of zeta's "x-ray" (Arias de Reyna, math/0309433). The Levinson–Montgomery equivalence became load-bearing in the *opposite* direction: Levinson's 1974 proof that ≥ 1/3 of zeros lie on the line (and Conrey's 2/5) works through the zeros of ζ′ near the line — the one place where an RH-equivalence machinery produced a landmark partial result. Garunkštis–Šimėnas tested the equivalence in the extended Selberg class, where RH can fail, and found the ζ ↔ ζ′ zero correspondence persists, showing the criterion is a soft function-theoretic fact (argument principle + functional equation), not an arithmetic one. Modern work (2202.12126 etc.) counts ζ′ zeros left of the line under/without RH.
**Status:** Open as a criterion; unconditionally, ζ′ has at most O(log T)-density anomalies; the equivalence itself fully proved.
**Assessment:** A relocation that turned out *instrumentally* valuable: it doesn't make RH easier (zeros of ζ′ are no more accessible than zeros of ζ), but the ζ′-side viewpoint fed directly into Levinson's method — the strongest unconditional "positive proportion" results we have. The difficulty reappears as controlling ζ′/ζ, i.e. the zeros again.

## 10. Salem's criterion

**Name:** Salem's integral-equation criterion.
**Authors/year:** R. Salem, "Sur une proposition équivalente à l'hypothèse de Riemann", C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 236 (1953), 1127–1128 (no free scan located; Gallica has CRAS but article-level link not resolved).
**Local files:** `Patkowski-2020-Salem-criteria.pdf` (arXiv:2003.00581), `Salem-new-equiv-2026.pdf` (arXiv:2604.15396, González–Negrín 2026).
**Statement:** For fixed 1/2 < σ < 1: ζ has no zeros on Re s = σ ⟺ the homogeneous integral equation ∫_0^∞ t^{σ−1}(e^{xt}+1)^{−1} f(t) dt = 0 (all x > 0) has no nontrivial bounded solution. RH ⟺ this holds for every σ ∈ (1/2, 1). (The Fermi–Dirac kernel's Mellin transform is Γ(s)ζ(s)(1−2^{1−s}).)
**Attack attempts:** Essentially none head-on — the criterion is a direct Wiener-Tauberian/completeness translation (kernel's Mellin transform vanishes iff nontrivial annihilator exists), sibling of Nyman–Beurling. Patkowski (2020) and González–Negrín (2026) generalized the kernel family and characterized which solution classes (e.g. f(t) = t^{iγ}) are excluded, producing new equivalent statements; the 2026 note shows RH ⟺ the equation has no solutions of pure imaginary power type. Its interest is mainly as the cleanest "completeness of a dilation family" statement with a physically meaningful (Fermi–Dirac) kernel, occasionally revisited by mathematical physicists.
**Status:** Open; equivalence proved, no partial-progress program exists.
**Assessment:** Pure relocation: non-vanishing of ζ(σ+it) is restated as injectivity of an integral operator whose symbol is ζ. Nothing about the operator is easier than the symbol. Notable only for elegance and for seeding the Nyman–Beurling circle.

## 11. Sondow–Dumitrescu and other minor monotonicity reformulations (brief)

## 12. The de Branges approach and the Conrey–Li refutation

**Name:** de Branges' Hilbert-spaces-of-entire-functions program.
**Authors/year:** L. de Branges, "Hilbert spaces of entire functions" (Prentice-Hall 1968); claimed proofs of RH/GRH: 1986 (J. Funct. Anal. paper era), 1992, 2004 (124-page web preprint), revised repeatedly through the 2017 "The Riemann Hypothesis" 89-page manuscript; "Apology for the proof of the Riemann hypothesis" (Purdue web document, 2003–, periodically rewritten).
**Refutation:** J. B. Conrey & Xian-Jin Li, "A note on some positivity conditions related to zeta and L-functions", IMRN 2000; arXiv:math/9812166 (1998).
**Local files:** `deBranges-2003-Apology.pdf`, `deBranges-2017-proof-riemann.pdf` (both from https://www.math.purdue.edu/~branges/), `Conrey-Li-1998-positivity-deBranges.pdf`.
**The machinery:** A de Branges space H(E) is built from an entire function E with |E(z̄)| < |E(z)| for Im z > 0; it is a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space of entire functions with kernel K(w,z) = [E(z)Ē(w) − Ē(z̄)E(w̄)]/[2πi(w̄−z)]. de Branges' theorems (in the lineage of his proof of the Bieberbach conjecture) say: if certain *positivity conditions* hold — e.g. (Theorem 1 of Conrey–Li's summary) if Re⟨F(z), F(z+i)⟩_{H(E)} ≥ 0 for all F with F, F(·+i) ∈ H(E), for the E manufactured from ξ(s) (so that zeros of E on Im z = −1/2 correspond to zeros of ζ on the critical line) — then the zeros lie on the required line: GRH would follow. The approach is a rigorous variant of Hilbert–Pólya: the positivity condition would make a certain shift operator's real part nonnegative, forcing spectrum on a line.
**Why it fails (Conrey–Li, precisely):** The implication "positivity ⟹ RH" is correct — but the hypothesis is false for the actual zeta and L-function spaces. Conrey–Li showed (i) the positivity condition forces, at every zero w of E, the local inequality Re{Ē′(w)E(w+i)/2πi} ≥ 0; (ii) for the space attached to the Riemann ξ-function (and for L(s, χ₄)), explicit computation at low zeros *violates* this necessary consequence — numerically, the quantities alternate in sign. In the functional-space version (their Theorem 2), the positivity of ⟨F, TF⟩ would force W(z)/W(z+i) to have nonnegative real part in Im z > −1/2, and again ζ-data refutes it. So de Branges' sufficient conditions are simply not satisfied by ζ: the program in its stated form cannot prove RH, independent of any gap-hunting in his manuscripts. de Branges' "Apology" (local PDF) responds by recasting the history (Stieltjes, Hermite, Fourier analysis on skew-planes) and asserting the criticisms address superseded formulations; his 2017 manuscript (local PDF, 89 pp.) claims a proof via a maximal dissipative transformation in weighted Hardy spaces; no part of it has been accepted, and the community position (Sarnak, Conrey) remains that the known counter-computations block every published variant that is precise enough to check.
**Status:** Approach considered refuted-as-stated since 1998; de Branges (b. 1932, retired from Purdue 2023) has continued revising his manuscripts for over two decades; no independent verification of any version has ever been produced.
**Assessment:** The most instructive failure in the subject: it is the unique modern program that produced *checkable sufficient conditions* for RH — and they turned out false. The lesson generalizes: any Hilbert-space positivity strong enough to imply RH through soft operator theory tends to assert *more* than RH (typically something like nonnegativity of Λ-coefficient sums or of Re at zeros), and ζ, which barely satisfies RH-consistent numerics, flunks the stronger condition. Positivity must be exactly calibrated — which is precisely what nobody knows how to do.

## 14. Recent (2015–2026) quantitative work through equivalences — roundup

- **Weil positivity:** Connes–Consani 2020–2022 (arXiv:2006.13771 + PNAS "UV prolate spectrum" 2022) — positivity proved semi-locally/archimedean via Sonin spaces; `Groskin-2026-truncated-Weil-form.pdf` (July 2026) on truncated quadratic forms. Most conceptually live front.
- **Nyman–Beurling:** Bettin–Conrey–Farmer asymptotic (conditional); Darses–Hillion 2020/22 polynomial-approximation NB variant; Ehm 2024 Gram-matrix formulae; cotangent-sum distribution theory (Maier–Rassias school).

- **Robin/Nicolas:** Morrill–Platt 20-free (2021), Axler 2021–23 sharper explicit bounds, verification to 10^{10^{13}}; Robin-type criteria for other arithmetic functions (Ramanujan J. papers).

## Synthesis: what equivalences have actually bought, and why difficulty is conserved

Historically, exactly two reformulation families have produced real leverage, and in both cases the leverage was *collateral*, not a march on RH itself. First, Weil positivity: in the function-field world the same reformulation *is* the proof (Weil, then Deligne via a different positivity), which is why it remains the only equivalence with a demonstrated capacity to finish the problem given the right ambient geometry; everything Connes, Deninger, and Consani have built is an attempt to manufacture that geometry over Z, and the 2020–2026 progress (archimedean-place positivity, prolate/Sonin spectral matching) is genuine mathematics even though the global inequality stands untouched. Second, Speiser/Levinson–Montgomery: routing the zero-counting through ζ′ is the mechanism inside Levinson's 1/3 and Conrey's 2/5 — the strongest unconditional statements toward RH we possess. A third, softer payoff is diagnostic: the Nyman–Beurling/Báez-Duarte program proved an unconditional 1/log N floor for d_N, and Arias de Reyna proved that Keiper–Li numerics can never certify more than the already-verified zero height; both are theorems *about the limits of attack routes*, which is real knowledge. Everything else — Robin/Lagarias/Nicolas, Riesz/Hardy–Littlewood/Báez-Duarte sequences, Redheffer, Franel–Landau, Salem, BSY/Volchkov — has yielded excellent explicit estimates, computations, and pedagogy, but the RH-critical exponent has not moved by an epsilon through any of them in a century.

The general phenomenon is difficulty conservation, and its signature is that *positivity always resurfaces*. Every equivalence, traced to its core, demands square-root cancellation in a Möbius- or Λ-weighted sum (M(x) ≪ x^{1/2+ε}, ψ(x) − x ≪ x^{1/2+ε}), and square-root cancellation is a positivity statement — of the Weil functional, of the λ_n, of a Gram matrix's smallest eigenvalue behavior, of an integral of log|ζ| that is secretly a sum of penalties over off-line zeros, of Re⟨F, F(·+i)⟩ in a de Branges space. Reformulations are lossless (that is what "equivalent" means), so they cannot destroy the difficulty, only transport it; what they change is which mathematical community's tools can grip the problem, and occasionally (function fields, Levinson) that transport reaches a toolbox that actually cuts. The de Branges episode is the boundary case that proves the rule: the moment a program states positivity conditions strong enough to be checkable, they turn out to demand more than RH and fail against the numerics — while conditions weak enough to be true retreat to being RH itself. The realistic reading, visible across all 20+ criteria above: equivalences are instruments for *understanding* the problem and for harvesting side-theorems, and the only one with a live claim to be more than that is Weil positivity, because it alone has a completed blueprint in another category.
